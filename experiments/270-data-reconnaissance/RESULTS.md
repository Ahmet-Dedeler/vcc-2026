## 1. GPSAdb 2.0 audit

### 1.1 What it is (sources: the NAR paper PMC12807611 / doi 10.1093/nar/gkaf1077; the site `https://www.gpsadb.com/`; figshare 29435822; Zenodo 17242712; the metadata table read from the site — `work/gpsadb_metadata_merged.csv`)
- **Content:** 7,665 perturbation groups (manually curated treat-vs-control pairs, ≥ 2 samples per group), 42,235 RNA-seq samples, 2,810 perturbed genes, 1,063 cell lines (metadata: 1,063 `CellLineName`, with Cellosaurus and DepMap ids), **4,623 distinct accessions** (6,821 groups from SRA/GEO, 844 from ENCODE). Human only ("restricted to *Homo sapiens*, expression profiling by high-throughput sequencing"). Methods (metadata `method`): **shRNA 2,848 · siRNA 2,371 · knockout 2,192 · inhibit 115 · CRISPRi 74 · mutation 65** — so ~68 % knockdown (shRNA/siRNA), ~29 % knockout, ~1 % CRISPRi; the paper lists CRISPRi/a and time courses as future additions.
- **Processing:** one Snakemake pipeline from SRA archives to DE (`xuzhougeng/auto_sra_rnaseq_pipeline` v1.0, Zenodo 17242712, CC BY 4.0). Each DE table (`work/gpsadb_D10001_diffresults.csv`, 33,532 genes) has `Dataset, gene_id, AveExpr, logFC, lfcSE, P.Value, adj.P.Val, gene` — a limma-style table with a standard error, so |logFC/lfcSE| is a Wald z. Groups were kept if both the up- and down-regulated sets had > 10 genes and the enrichment count ≥ 2.
- **Access and terms:** site "freely accessible without registration", footer **CC BY 4.0**; the bulk DE archive `GPSAdiffResults.zip` on figshare (8,306,455,823 bytes, md5 `3fa7fa26…`, **CC BY 4.0**, 6,245 members `Dxxxxx_diffresults.csv`, 20.9 GB uncompressed) — **6,244 of the 7,665 groups have a DE table in the archive**, one member is not in the metadata. The paper text is CC BY-NC (OUP). Metadata tables ("Detailed Metadata", "Cell Annotation", GTF) are Shiny download handlers that never registered in a headless session (the anchor is hidden, the output stays suspended, the download URL 404s); the same data is rendered as server-side DataTables on the Download and Browse tabs, which I read out in full (7,665 rows each; `work/gpsadb_metadata_detail.csv` = per-group DE/GSEA/PCA summaries, `work/gpsadb_metadata_browse.csv` = accession/Cellosaurus/DepMap ids). A "programmatic interface" is mentioned for fastGPSA only; no documented REST API was found.
- **Provenance caveat the paper itself states:** representation is uneven across lines and platforms; knockdown efficiency and off-targets vary; the authors rank within dataset for that reason.

### 1.3 The frozen feature (`work/FEATURE-DEFINITION.md`, sha256 `f8a6bd39b48ca1ad7ce910b07115894312349ba211c95fec04d4cf1450fd56cb`, written 13:22 UTC before any DE table was read)
`SS_cross(t)` = mean over pairs of groups of t from DIFFERENT accessions of the sign agreement of `logFC` on the union of the two groups' top-100 genes by |logFC/lfcSE| (target excluded, zero/NA dropped, pair used if ≥ 20 genes in the union are measured in both). Chance 0.5. Declared secondaries: `SS_line` (cross-line pairs), `RHO_cross` (Spearman of logFC on the union), N = 50 / 200 and |logFC|-ranked variants, `SS_within_study`. Tests (frozen): Spearman with the mean cancer-fold `cd4svd5` `pds` (paired target bootstrap B = 2,000, permutation p) and AUC for rest-vs-anti-aligned (262's base·truth ≤ 0, majority over folds); decision rule: both intervals must exclude chance.

Post-hoc diagnostics (NOT part of the frozen rule, labelled as such): partial ρ(`SS_cross`, `pds` | log n_studies) **+0.374 [+0.012, +0.634]** — the feature is uncorrelated with the study count (ρ +0.08), so the popularity control does not explain it; within strata ρ +0.34 (≥ 3 studies, n 23) and +0.38 (exactly 2, n 17). The 4 anti-aligned targets are CAT, CDK8, ZKSCAN3, PHF19 at `SS_cross` 0.43–0.47 against a rest median of 0.54; the 7 targets at ≥ 0.60 (AKAP1, ANP32B, BCL6, CARM1, PAPOLA, SCAF8, EPHB2) all sit at `pds` ≥ 0.71, five at ≥ 0.99. Reading. By the letter of the pre-written rule the primary separates (both intervals exclude chance). By the evidence it is a weak, fragile positive: the ρ interval touches zero; the AUC rests on four targets; the statistic averages 0.52 against 0.50, so most of its variance is noise on 100-gene unions; the two declared secondaries that change the ranking key (|logFC|, `RHO_cross`) lose the ρ interval; and the subset is famous, strongly-responding genes. Its within-study sibling (same laboratory, different line or reagent; agreement 0.79) carries no signal about `pds`, so the informative part is not "the response is reproducible" but "the response survives a change of study" — protocol, depth and line together. That is compatible with 262's diagnosis (transferability is a target property tied to cross-line agreement, ρ +0.63 measured on our own lines), but this feature is a much noisier reading of it (ρ +0.09 with that covariate). **Status: a candidate covariate for 269 on ≤ 40 targets, to be confirmed on held targets before any use; no closure and no go at this scope.** Multiplicity: the primary was one pre-declared test pair; the six secondaries and the CMap cells (14) are the screen around it and are reported in full.

## 3. PerturbFate (GEO GSE291147; Nature 2026, doi 10.1038/s41586-026-10367-0, PMC13233327; bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.05.02.651939; code JunyueCaoLab/PerturbFate)

- **System and design (paper full text, Europe PMC):** A375 melanoma (BRAF V600E), dCas9-KRAB CRISPRi, dual-sgRNA library; **143 candidate genes** chosen from prior bulk vemurafenib-resistance CRISPR screens (FDR < 0.1) and filtered to ≥ 1 log2 CPM in A375 (Supplementary Table 2); **155 sgRNA pairs incl. NO-TARGET** (sgRNA matrices, `work/perturbfate_targets.json`); **119 perturbations retained** after sgRNA-efficiency and cell-number filters; conditions DMSO vs vemurafenib (1 µM inhibits ~90 % growth); nascent RNA by **5-ethynyl-uridine, 1 h** labelling, click-biotinylation and streptavidin pull-down.
- **Modalities and cells:** dual-omics = chromatin accessibility + steady-state RNA (sgRNA matrix 155 × **78,510** cells; ED Fig: 25,671 DMSO + 21,233 Vem QC-passed); tri-omics = ATAC + nascent RNA + pre-existing RNA (155 × **158,432** cells; 60,905 DMSO + 49,253 Vem). Averages of 438 tri-omic / 214 dual-omic cells per perturbation (DMSO) and 353 / 177 (Vem). "Most perturbations showed consistent knockdown across chromatin accessibility, nascent and steady-state transcriptomes."
- **Processed deposition (GEO FTP `suppl/`, sizes from the listing):** Dual-omics ATAC peak matrix h5ad 2.3 GB + cell metadata 3.8 MB; Dual-omics RNA gene-count RDS 302 MB + metadata 1.1 MB; sgRNA count RDS 1.9 MB; **LabeledRNA (nascent) RDS 403 MB; UnlabeledRNA (pre-existing) RDS 663 MB**; Tri-omics ATAC h5ad 3.8 GB + metadata 6.5 MB, RNA metadata 5.8 MB, sgRNA RDS 2.8 MB. Public on 2026-02-06; the cell-metadata CSVs carry only UMI/gene counts, condition and (tri-omics) `raw_nascent_ratio` — the target identity is in the sgRNA matrices (parsed here). Overlap with the panel: **6 / 300 — ANP32B, CAND1, CDK8, MED13, MED15, SUPT7L.** Overlap with our donor/challenge lines: none (A375 is a CMap line, not a donor).
- **One narrow question it can answer:** *for a target whose knockdown response fails to transfer, is the primary response transcriptional (nascent) and only its steady-state readout context-dependent, or is the steady-state change itself post-transcriptional (abundance/stability), which no basal-state model of transcription could predict?* Cheapest test: download the two RNA RDS files + sgRNA matrix (~1.07 GB, under the cap), pseudobulk each of the 119 perturbations vs NO-TARGET in DMSO for nascent and pre-existing layers, and for the 6 panel targets compare the nascent-vs-pre-existing top-gene sign agreement with 262's group label and with the CMap cross-line stability — a 6-target case study (≈ 30 min, < 4 GB). It cannot test a correlation at n = 6, so it stays a mechanism note; the general version (nascent-vs-steady-state agreement as a per-target feature) would need a multi-target nascent screen that covers the panel, which does not exist.

What this changes for the programme: the "cross-study sign stability" idea has now been measured on the two resources that can provide it — on GPSAdb (40 replicated targets) it is a weak positive that survives a popularity control but rests on 4 anti-aligned targets and a chance-level mean agreement, and on CMap (113 targets, up to 13 lines each) it reads at chance against 262's per-target `pds` and its anti-aligned group. Under rule 6 of the programme, that closes **top-100 sign agreement ACROSS LINES on L1000 signatures as a predictor of per-target transferability in our pipeline** (0/6 cells on 82–113 targets); it does NOT close the bulk cross-study construction, which is neither established nor closed at n = 40, and it does not touch the idea that transferability is a per-target biological property (262's held-vs-donor agreement, ρ +0.63, is exactly that, measured on our own lines). The two signals worth following are cross-study survival of the bulk signature (GPSAdb, 40 targets) and reagent-independence within a line (CMap, 47 targets); both are reliability measures, both belong to 267/269, and both need a held-target confirmation before anything is built on them.
