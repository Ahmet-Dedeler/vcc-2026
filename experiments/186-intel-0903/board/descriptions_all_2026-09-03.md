A correction first. Our entry Prism described a rank-20 Gram deflation. It was never applied: the build script declared the flag and never called the routine, so Prism holds only the axes-4 projection and the sign-gated orphan rows. The local numbers were real; the package was not what the text said. This is our first entry that whitens at all.

Mechanism. Perturbation discrimination ranks each prediction against every target's measurement, so rows that resemble one another lose however faithful each is alone. We unit-normalise the 300 rows in the metric's own pseudobulk space, remove the panel centroid, and apply a Tikhonov inverse of the row Gram, (G + lambda * mean(s^2) * I)^-1 with lambda 0.01: a smooth 1/s^2 gain per direction instead of a step at rank 20. Rows are rescaled to their original norms, so fold-change error keeps its units and the discrimination gain is untouched. Measured on three held-out recipient lines (HCT116, HEK293T, primary CD4 T cells) with leave-one-out voting, the ridge beats the rank-20 cut on all three and lifts discrimination 0.044 on the official scale over no whitening, flat in lambda from 0.001 to 0.1. The sign gate now also hears a genome-wide primary T-cell CRISPRi screen and the KOLF2.1J iPSC atlas.

The trade is real: against a held-out-control harness this gains 0.030 discrimination and loses 0.011 direction fidelity and 0.010 fold-change error, both significant. Whitening rotates each row away from its own truth to separate it from the others.

What this is for. Whitening has never been on this board from us, so its live transfer is unmeasured, while the voters' class transfers near 0.6. The readout was fixed before upload: discrimination beyond 0.63 x 0.0119 is attributed to whitening and divided by 0.0435. If that ratio is near zero, whitening does not travel across cell lines and it leaves our final entry.
**prev desc (Aug28):** Same predictions as our previous entry; only their layout across the 400 cells of each group changes.

Direction fidelity is k / max(n_pred, n_real), so a submission is charged for calling fewer genes than the measurement found. We extracted the differential expression our own previous entry produces and compared it against the call set it intends. Of the 5,601 genes it means to assert, its cells make only 2,226 significant. What separates the two groups is not confidence: their directional accuracy is 0.516 against 0.518, and the genes that fail carry the larger predicted fold change. It is expression. Genes that clear the test sit at a median 107 counts per million in control, those that fail at 38.7. At this depth a 38 CPM gene averages under one count per cell, so its column is almost all ties at zero, and a rank test cannot separate what is not there. This also explains a null we had not accounted for: cutting the dispersion of called genes fivefold changes nothing, because shrinking the spread of a column of zeros moves no rank.

The emitter was holding every entry its drawn control cell had at zero, so the submission's sparsity matched the control pool's. That is what keeps the sparse half of the call set from being expressed at all. Releasing it lets a called gene's fixed pseudobulk mass spread across more of its cells, the one channel a rank test reads on a sparse gene. Genes outside the call set keep their zeros regardless.
