## Abstract

Predicting a perturbation's effect in an unseen cell context requires three quantities the literature conflates: measurement reliability, sharing across contexts, and transfer. On 300 non-essential CRISPRi targets we separate them and find them far apart, in three measurements on different cells: within-reference split-cell repeatability +0.243 (H1, 85 targets), across-donor agreement +0.054 (primary CD4 T cells, 297 targets) and a cross-line median transfer of +0.020 (300 panel targets). We then decompose a perturbation measurement into its experimental units on one matched design. Cells and sequencing depth are one removable axis obeying Spearman–Brown; the batch is free (ρ 0.87–1.12); a different study in the same line costs little (0.79); a different line costs about half (0.35–0.45); and the guide construct is the dominant irreducible term — ρ 0.09–0.41 on public CRISPRi libraries as measured, ≈ 0.03–0.08 once a cross-gene null is subtracted, and **+0.075 [−0.010, +0.288]** in the challenge's own library, where it is mostly knockdown failure; on public libraries guide efficacy explains it in immortalised lines but not in primary T cells. Above 76–330 cells per side, more measurement of one construct says nothing further about the gene. Against that floor we report a reproducible cross-line conservation map that predicts transfer with the recipient held out (ρ +0.387) and transfers to three surfaces outside its own banks, including eight untouched datasets (ρ +0.50, preregistered); a preregistered family axis from core machinery to cell identity, confirmed on 17,106 held genes; a per-gene remainder annotation cannot predict that nevertheless reproduces across disjoint source sets (+0.328); and a response-geometry rule whose preregistered sign inverted. Turning to what a construct contributes beyond its intended gene, we **re-measure a published phenomenon on a new sample** — the benchmark's own 2025 CRISPRi library, whose specificity its producers did not assess — with the construct as the estimand's unit (n = 34): an aggregate association between the genes a construct's protospacer seed matches in their promoters and the repression of those genes in that construct's own cells, **S = +0.713 [+0.324, +1.074]** standardised units per construct against a selection-matched joint null (p = 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ — a *gene-sampling* p whose 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ floor is a property of the 100,000 draws, not of the 34 units), while the identical genes read at background level in the cells of the *partner* construct that knocks the same gene down with different protospacers, which is the field's standard specificity control and not ours. That attaches the association to the protospacer sequence; it does not establish off-target binding — already shown by dCas9 ChIP-seq in this library's parent collection — and the two analyses behind it are two tests of one hypothesis on one data set. Finally we show why a rank metric rewards exactly the directions in which a fitted whitener has learnt its own training noise. On the benchmark's own scorer, the fold-change members reward sign information on weak and uncalled responses, and none of
the public sources we could assemble supplies it across cancer lines: target-specific combinations of six same-target rows
from four public screens gain +0.016 [+0.007, +0.024] in pooled sign accuracy on the stem-cell reference and −0.0002 …
+0.0033 on three held-out cancer lines. On the stem-cell reference the one source that closed most of that gap was a screen
of the same line from another laboratory. **[[PENDING 09-30:

## Plain-language summary

If you switch off a gene in a cell, the cell's other genes change how much they are read out. We wanted to know three separate things: how reliably that change can be measured, how much of it is the same in a different kind of cell, and how much can therefore be predicted for a cell type nobody has measured. Working from twenty-eight public experiments and one benchmark competition's own data, we found that these questions have very different answers, and that people often quote one as if it answered another. The most useful practical result is uncomfortable: once you have measured a few hundred cells, the biggest remaining disagreement between two measurements of "the same" perturbation is not noise, not the batch, and not even the cell type — it is which CRISPR guide was used. Two guides aimed at the same gene disagree almost as much as two unrelated perturbations. In some libraries much of that turns out to be guides that simply fail to switch the gene off — roughly one guide in five to one in four does not — and a failed guide still produces a repeatable change, just the wrong one; in primary T cells the disagreement is there even when both guides work. Either way it caps how well a new screen can be predicted from old ones, because the new screen uses its own guides. Part of what a guide does besides its intended job turns out to be readable from its own sequence. In the competition's 2025 library, genes whose promoters happen to match the short recognition end of a guide are, on average, more switched off in the cells carrying that guide than carefully matched genes that do not match it — and the same genes are *not* switched off in cells carrying a different guide aimed at the same gene. That ties the effect to the sequence rather than to the intended target. It is an association measured on thirty-four guides in one experiment, not proof that the guide sticks to those genes — and it is not a new discovery. The effect, the sequence rule and the two-guide control are all in the published literature; one of those papers watched guides physically stick to the wrong promoters in the very library ours descends from, two years earlier. What is new here is only that it has now been measured on this particular library, which nobody had checked. An earlier draft of this paper called for a new laboratory experiment to settle the mechanism; that call is withdrawn, because the mechanism was already settled elsewhere. In the benchmark we measured it against, that cap is real but still some way above where the best current methods sit. What *does* transfer is a gene-level property we can measure in advance: some perturbations produce broad, generic responses that look similar in every cell line, and others produce narrow, cell-type-specific ones. Core cellular machinery is in the first group; genes that define what kind of cell a cell is are in the second. That distinction is reproducible, partly predictable from annotation alone, and it survives being tested in cell lines and assay types it was never fitted on — including eight datasets we had deliberately never opened until the test was written down.

Since the last revision we asked a narrower question about the competition itself: in a cell line nobody has perturbed,
which way do the many weakly affected genes move? Every public source we could combine either fails outright or helps on
the stem-cell reference and not on the cancer lines we could test. That includes other laboratories' screens of the same
genes, the cell line's own unperturbed cells, and technical features of the measurement. The one thing that carried the
direction, on the stem-cell line, was another laboratory's screen of that same line, and the competition's cell lines have
no such public screen. **[[PENDING 09-30: whether the organisers' own screen of a different cell line carries it.]]**

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The organising question

> Which perturbation responses are conserved across cell states, which depend on the recipient's state, and when can a model know that its transfer prediction is unreliable? (`docs/RESEARCH-PROGRAMME-2026-09.md`)

The question is forced on the field by a practical task. Genome-scale Perturb-seq now exists in many cell lines, and the obvious use for it is to predict the response of a perturbation in a context where it was never measured. Whether that is possible at all depends on quantities that are usually reported one at a time, on different data, with different estimators — and then compared as if they were the same number.

### 1.2 Three quantities the literature conflates

A "reproducibility" number attached to a perturbation screen can mean at least three different things.

1. **Within-reference split-cell repeatability.** Split one perturbation's cells in one experiment into two halves and compare the two response estimates. This is what the challenge's own score-1 anchor computes ([VCC-SPEC]) and what most quality-control figures report.
2. **Across-replicate or across-donor agreement.** The same perturbation, same cell type and assay, in two biological replicates — two people, say. This is what the GWCD4i authors publish as `donor_correlation_all_mean` ([GWCD4i-TAB]).
3. **Across-context transfer.** The same perturbation in two different cell contexts. This is what a zero-shot prediction has to survive, and what [TRANSPORT26] formalises as causal transportability.

In the same residual space, but on different cells (H1, 85 targets; CD4 T cells, 297; the cross-line map, 300), these read **+0.243**, **+0.054** and **+0.020** (§3(a), §3(b)) — three quantities, not three estimates of one, roughly twelve to one apart across the range. **Stated cleanly, this separation is the single most useful thing in this paper**, and it is the point the 287 audit identified as more valuable than any individual number in the programme.

Two definitions we use throughout. A **response** is a pseudobulk log1p(CPM) profile of the perturbed cells minus that side's controls; a **residual** response subtracts the mean over the source's own panel rows, so what is compared is a target's direction rather than the screen's global tone (Methods §5.2). A **unit** is the thing a split resamples: cells, UMIs, a batch, a guide construct, a donor, an activation state, a cell line, a study. Every number below names its unit, because a number that does not is uninterpretable.

### 1.3 The motivating setting

The 2026 Virtual Cell Challenge asks for the post-perturbation expression of **300 CRISPRi targets at 400 cells per perturbation in six cell lines on 10x Flex**, zero-shot: no perturbed cell from any 2026 context is released (`data/challenge/pert_counts.csv`; `manifest.json`). Each target is realised by **one construct**. Three of the six contexts (A/B/C) are held cancer lines.

One fact about that design turned out to control the whole transfer question, and we checked it directly: the 2026 panel shares only **25 of its 300 targets** with the 2025 H1 library (train + validation + test, 300 targets). No source on disk carries the 2026 constructs for any target. Whatever a construct contributes to a truth — and §3(a) shows it contributes most of what survives cell-sampling noise — is therefore unavailable to every zero-shot entrant equally.

### 1.4 What this paper is, and is not

It is a synthesis of one measurement programme: sixteen findings recorded in `docs/FINDINGS-2026-09.md` (F1–F16), nine preregistered confirmation claims logged in `docs/CONFIRMATION-LEDGER.md` (C1–C9), and a 24-row novelty audit against the source papers and the transfer literature (`NOVELTY-AUDIT.md`). It is not a benchmark paper and does not propose a model; numbers from the competition's scorer are engineering calibration, not biology: they are confined to §3(h)–(l) and the Discussion, and §3(i)–(l) use them only as evidence about what the scorer's members measure. Under programme rule 10 every claim below carries a provenance verdict — the audit graded its 24 items **NEW 4, EXTENDED 11, KNOWN 7, CONTRADICTED 2**, with one contradiction subsequently reconciled, and a sixteenth finding that postdated it (F16, §3(g)) was audited separately at **NEW 0, EXTENDED 1, KNOWN 4, CONTRADICTED 0** — and where a claim postdates an audit and has no verdict, we say so rather than silently claiming novelty.

## 2. Data and instruments

Four measurement surfaces carry everything below.

- **28 public perturbation banks over 21 cell lines** (`E276/work/inventory276_sources.csv`), the substrate for the conservation map and the family axis.
- **PLOLO** — leave-one-library-out over the panel, with the held line's own 150–200-cell pseudobulk as truth (K562, HCT116, HEK293T held). Its truths have split-half residual reliability 0.05–0.09, so only fold totals and group aggregates are readable on it (§3(a).
- **The h1-test instrument** — 85 targets × 400 cells of H1 hESC on 10x Flex, scored with the challenge scorer's own per-target rank statistic, which reproduces `cell-eval2`'s `pds_cosine` on a cloud-scored artifact **to 5.6 × 10⁻¹⁷**.
- **Broad-16** — 82 Cas9 knockouts × 12 working cancer lines as pseudobulk halves, plus four reserved lines scored exactly once.
- **The 2025 challenge library's own cells**, the surface §3(g) is measured on — 65 constructs (34 gene-targeting, covering 17 paired intended targets, plus 31 non-targeting) across three splits that share one pool of **38,176** control cells, read only through saved integer sufficient sums, with the constructs' protospacers recovered from the public parent library.

Reserved data is scored once per preregistered claim and logged. Three reserves have been declared and all three are now spent: the Orion HEK293T half-B truths (267), four Broad-16 lines (268, spent by 283), and **R3** — eight scPerturb files that no bank, map row, donor table or fold had touched, 174 held-map genes pooled, declared from metadata only (292) and scored once, in a 28-second access, for claim C5 (295).

## 3. Results

### 3(a) What a single-cell perturbation reference resolves

**F1 — a 150–200-cell response on a non-essential panel is mostly not reproducible; the challenge's own Flex-400 reference is. Provenance: F1a KNOWN, F1b EXTENDED, F1c KNOWN. Confirmation: no separate ledger claim; the HEK293T half-B reliability was the single access that defined reserve R1.**

At 70–96 cells per half, the median split-half residual cosine of a panel target's response is **+0.047** on K562-GWPS (gemgroup parity, 269 targets), **+0.028** on Orion HCT116 (batch parity, 290) and **+0.028** on Orion HEK293T (batch parity, 294), with **5.6 % / 32 % / 34 %** of targets at or below zero. On the challenge's own 2025 H1 reference — 10x Flex, 200 cells per half, 85 targets — the same statistic reads **+0.243** [p10 +0.067, p90 +0.748] with **no target at or below zero**.

This is a re-measurement. X-Atlas/Orion's own preprint reports 48 / 40 / 54 % of on-target perturbations (HCT116 / HEK293T / K562) with a detectable single-cell change [ORION]; Replogle 2022 reports 31.1 % with a significant phenotype [REPLOGLE22]; and Wang et al. 2026 make the claim at scale — *"Among 7,170 perturbations from 29 datasets, 65% are unreliable"* — by split-half Pearson with a Spearman–Brown correction, on a superset of our data [WANG26]. What is ours is the conditioning: a residual cosine, restricted to the challenge's 300 non-essential targets, at a stated cells-per-half. One trap has misled readers of these datasets: Orion's headline **median Spearman 0.99 is NTC pseudobulk profile stability, not response reproducibility**.

The split unit matters where batches carry structure: HEK293T reads +0.028 by batch and **+0.071 by random cells** on the same 294 targets. Reliability is a property of the target, not of the cells spent on it — Spearman(reliability, cells per target) is **+0.16 / +0.09 / +0.12 / −0.05** across the four references against **+0.58 / +0.23 / +0.17 / +0.62** with the incumbent's per-target rank score. One inherited correction: "0.481 measured at 400 cells", quoted in three project documents, was a **linear-law extrapolation**; at 200/200 cells the measured split-half residual cosine has median +0.243 and mean +0.331.

**F14 — cells and UMI depth are one removable axis; the guide construct is the dominant irreducible term. Provenance (297, the audit's extension): the construct as the dominant term **KNOWN** (the CD4 source's own `guide_correlation_all` column reads +0.06–0.07 — our CD4 ρ_guide from the same screen; Replogle 2022's dual-sgRNA design exists to raise efficacy; Mixscale exists to weight cells by knockdown); the Spearman–Brown reliability law **KNOWN** (Wang et al. 2026 publish it as ρ(N) = Nτ²/(Nτ² + 1) on 29 datasets — ours verifies it on real cell ladders); the line / state / donor ladder **KNOWN** within a factor of ~1.5 of published counterparts; cells-and-depth-as-one-axis **EXTENDED** (expected for a pseudobulk statistic; the sequencing-budget literature's opposite headline is for per-cell objectives — T6); batch free **EXTENDED**; **NEW**: a different study in the same line agrees better than a second construct in the same study (a reliability ratio, not comparable with Replogle 2022's raw same-line cross-experiment r 0.50 without saying so — T8). Confirmation: preregistered (hash recorded 2026-09-06 13:54:51Z with only file metadata read); no ledger claim, since it is measured on non-reserved data.**

285 measured, on one matched design — the same targets, the same cells per side, in residual cosine and in the scorer's own statistic — which disagreement between two measurements of one perturbation more measurement can remove.

*Cells and depth are the same axis — for pseudobulk response reproducibility* (for per-cell objectives the sequencing-budget literature's headline is the opposite, T6). At equal total UMIs, 200 cells at half depth and 100 at full depth differ by **+0.0078 [−0.0068, +0.0119]** on h1-test and by +0.0012 / −0.0017 on K562; thinned ladders lie on the cell ladders (Figure F-1b). *And the cell axis obeys Spearman–Brown* — the reliability law Wang et al. 2026 publish as ρ(N) = Nτ²/(Nτ² + 1) [WANG26]; ours verifies it on real cell ladders: extrapolating from the n = 25 rung, measured minus predicted is **−0.006 [−0.030, +0.033]** on h1-test (25 → 200: predicted +0.2455, measured +0.2392), −0.006 on K562, −0.004 on Jurkat, −0.018 on HepG2 (Figure F-1a) — the replacement for a depth curve retracted earlier in the programme (below).

*The construct is the dominant irreducible term.* On the 748 K562-GWPS perturbations that carry two constructs, at 25 cells per side, two constructs of the same gene agree at **+0.0006 [−0.0002, +0.0014]** (48 % at or below zero) against **+0.0063 [+0.0055, +0.0071]** for two halves of one construct's cells; the paired difference CELLS − GUIDE is **+0.0058 [+0.0045, +0.0069]**. In the scorer's own statistic the same swap takes mean `pds` from **0.633 to 0.509** against a chance value of 0.500 — very nearly a random relabelling as far as the challenge's metric is concerned. The effect replicates on Nadig Jurkat (`pds` 0.800 → 0.560), Nadig HepG2 and Broad-16 (pooled +0.0022 [+0.0009, +0.0032] over 9 of 12 lines).

Two corrections from 296 must be read with this table. First, in the four CRISPRi libraries the between-construct agreement is **not separated from a cross-gene null at the median** — a second construct of the same gene agrees no better than a construct of a different gene once each library's shared programme is accounted for ((b) − (x) intervals through zero; only CD4's clears) — so HepG2's 0.41 comes from a growth-arrest programme its essential targets share, and **null-corrected the ordering is K562 ≈ HepG2 ≈ CD4 ≈ 0.03–0.06, Jurkat 0.08, Broad-16 0.79**: the one construct term that survives its own null is the Cas9 library's (its rank statistic 0.70 against a null of 0.50). F14's cross-library ordering therefore partly ranked shared biology, not construct-specific biology. Second, in every CRISPRi library on disk a "construct" is a transcription-start-site unit (P1 versus P2 promoter constructs: 713 of 748 K562 pairs), not two guides against one transcript. The one new contrast in this table is the inversion in its middle rows: **changing the laboratory experiment costs less than changing the guide** (297). Every other row has a published counterpart — the construct term is the CD4 source's own column and the field's standing nuisance; the ladder is a harmonisation of five units on one statistic and one design, which is a service, not a discovery. The study ratio 0.79 is a Spearman–Brown-corrected reliability ratio, not Replogle 2022's raw r of 0.50 for the same line pair (T8) — and the inversion survives the translation into raw units (298): on the same 112 K562 perturbations at full depth, a different study agrees at raw cosine **+0.168 [+0.120, +0.215]** and a second construct of the same gene at **+0.011 [+0.004, +0.018]**, a 16-fold gap (+0.157 [+0.108, +0.206]); the pooled study raw cosine of +0.215 reaches +0.411 on the strongest-responding third, which is where Replogle's ≈ 0.50 on a selected cohort sits, while the construct term is flat with magnitude (+0.006 in every tertile).

Better guides raise the construct ceiling without abolishing it: ρ moves 0.12 → 0.24 across terciles of on-target knockdown, and knockdown strength predicts the cell-split agreement as strongly as the guide agreement — the mechanism is response magnitude, not off-target cleanliness. Solving the Spearman–Brown law for the n at which cell-sampling attenuation falls to the construct's ceiling gives **89 cells per side (Jurkat), 76 (HepG2), 328 (h1-test)**: above that, a second 400-cell measurement of the *same* construct keeps improving while a measurement of the *same gene* through another construct does not. Reproducibility at fixed n likewise tracks magnitude, not cells: h1-test terciles read +0.044 / +0.140 / +0.506, and in one file at one n the K562 *panel* targets read +0.0095 against +0.0605 for K562 *essential* targets.

**The same measurement in the challenge's own regime (293).** On the 2025 H1 library itself — Arc's dual-guide design, 10x Flex, 17 two-construct targets each measured inside its own split's residual space, thinned to the challenge's depth (19,965 UMIs per cell against the 2026 release's 20,340) — **ρ_guide reads +0.075 [−0.010, +0.288]** at 200 cells per side, flat across an 8× range of cells and a 2.7× range of depth. Extrapolated by Spearman–Brown to 400 cells, a 400-cell truth agrees with another of the **same** construct at **+0.476 [+0.260, +0.717]** and with a **different** construct of the same gene at **+0.046 [−0.006, +0.121]**. At the median the two-construct agreement is not separated from a cross-target null (**+0.0067 [−0.0333, +0.0825]**), though the rank statistic still separates them (`pds` 1.000 → 0.677 against a cross-target 0.539). Lanes are free here too. F14 holds in the challenge's own library, at the low end of the CRISPRi range.

**But the term is largely knockdown failure, not construct-specific biology.** Stratifying the 16 targets by the *weaker* construct's own on-target knockdown — a column excluded from every scored statistic — ρ_guide moves from **−0.003 [−0.085, +0.181]** (11 targets whose weaker construct does not reach |lfc| 1.0) to **+0.687 [+0.197, +1.067]** (5 targets where both do); the weaker construct's knockdown correlates with the per-target ratio at Spearman +0.626 (p = 0.009). Restricting instead to pairs whose second construct is merely *measurable* does not rescue it (+0.067): the failing constructs are not silent — HIRA_P1 (lfc +0.030) and MED15_P2 (−0.053) miss their genes and still deliver split-half-reproducible transcriptomes (+0.268, +0.047) that disagree with their knocking-down partners. And two-construct targets are not a random sample: their 34 constructs knock down significantly worse than the library's 283 single-construct targets (38.2 % vs 16.6 % below |lfc| 1.0, Mann–Whitney p = 0.0015). **So +0.075 is a lower bound on the library-wide construct term, and +0.687 is the estimate that applies to the ~83 % of targets whose one construct works.** **Tested on the public libraries (296; preregistered), the answer is partly.** Stratified by the weaker construct's knockdown at a library-relative cut, Nadig Jurkat splits cleanly (ρ_guide **+0.458 [+0.245, +1.180]** where both constructs knock down against **+0.096 [−0.175, +0.210]** where the weaker fails, at all three cuts and both rungs; knockdown predicts the construct agreement at +0.446 and the cell split at −0.022); K562-GWPS splits at n = 50 (**+0.340 [+0.113, +0.548]** against +0.001 [−0.082, +0.105]) but knockdown there predicts the cell split as strongly as the guide split, so magnitude rides along; HepG2's point estimates split (+0.645 against +0.307) with an interval through zero; Broad-16 (Cas9) shows no split (+0.951 against +0.763 — its constructs work whatever the mRNA readout says); and **primary CD4 T cells show no split at all**, at any condition, budget or cut, with 41–199 pairs per stratum (+0.018 against +0.014) — a powered null. So "guide efficacy is the dominant irreducible term" is not a general claim; "in immortalised-line CRISPRi libraries a validated construct pair agrees much better than an unvalidated one, and in primary T cells it does not" is, with Arc's own 2025 library as the favourable case. And a construct that fails still gives a reproducible transcriptome **at scale**: for 2,400+ constructs with |lfc| < 0.5, the own split-half interval is above zero in all five libraries (K562 +0.0069, Jurkat +0.035, HepG2 +0.138, Broad-16 +0.091, CD4 +0.104) — systematically less reproducible than working constructs, no closer to the library's mean response, and no more similar to other failing constructs than working ones are to each other: **reproducible, and not a shared vector signature**.

**F10b — the biological donor is a marginal replicate unit, and replicates beat cells at a matched budget. Provenance: F10a KNOWN, F10b NEW. Confirmation: preregistered (hash 2026-09-06 06:57:35Z, before any expression value was read); no ledger claim.**

### 3(b) The cross-line conservation map

**F8 — a target's cross-line conservation is a reproducible property that predicts its transfer. Provenance: EXTENDED. Confirmation: claim C1 in the ledger, RELABELLED as split-half robustness, not an untouched confirmation.**

For each of the 300 panel targets, define **C(t)** as the mean, over all pairs of sources from *different* cell lines that both carry t, of the cosine between the target's residual response directions (frozen definition: `E276/work/DEFINITION-276.md`, hashed before any cosine was computed; same-line pairs are excluded from the primary because they measure reproducibility, not conservation). Nineteen lines contribute; 295 targets get a score.

*It reproduces* (Figure F-3a). Across independent cells of four sources, two estimates of the same target's conservation agree at Spearman **ρ +0.273 [+0.158, +0.388]** (n = 295). Across two **disjoint sets of cell lines**, they agree at **ρ +0.435 [+0.340, +0.522]** (n = 289); all 50 random line splits are positive, median +0.394.

*It predicts transfer with the recipient held out* (Figure F-3b). Read as bands, the bottom decile of C has mean `pds` 0.647 with 36.5 % of its targets anti-aligned, against 0.895 and 4.7 % in the top decile.

*It transfers off its own map* (291, claims C3/C4). The surfaces span two cell-line panels, an assay the map never saw and a different perturbation modality.

*What is extended, not new.* A per-target cross-cell-type conservation score **already exists in a published supplementary table**, with its own random-effect null ([GWCD4i-TAB]: median `logfc_pearson_r` +0.303 / +0.315 / +0.312 by condition against random controls at +0.004). Measuring it directly (288): within tertiles of an independent magnitude proxy, ρ(C, per-target `pds`) is **at chance in the weakest tertile** (+0.03 [−0.18, +0.24] on K562), +0.21…+0.28 in the middle and +0.33…+0.47 in the strongest; the partial Spearman given magnitude is **+0.26 / +0.25 / +0.23** against a raw +0.41 / +0.39 / +0.37. ### 3(c) The family axis and the per-gene remainder

**F9 — cell-identity gene families have less conserved perturbation responses than core-machinery families. Provenance: EXTENDED. Confirmation: preregistered (hash 2026-09-06 06:25:54Z, before any held number) and CONFIRMED by its own frozen rule on 17,106 held genes.**

The preregistered test asked whether zinc-finger transcription factors have lower cross-line conservation than other perturbations, with a pass rule requiring both a raw and an expression/magnitude-matched median difference to be negative with 95 % intervals below zero. On every non-panel gene measured in at least two lines on disk (**17,106 genes**, of which 696 zinc fingers), the raw difference is **Δmedian −0.01047 [−0.01269, −0.00844]**, AUC 0.564 [0.548, 0.581], one-sided Mann–Whitney p = 4.1 × 10⁻⁹; the matched difference is **−0.00635 [−0.01172, −0.00079]**. Both arms fire. None of 100 size-matched random families reaches the effect (null band [−0.0030, +0.0045]). It holds inside a single dataset (Orion HCT116 × HEK293T: −0.00901 [−0.01573, −0.00091]) and doubles on the balanced three-source core, where every gene contributes identical source pairs (−0.01785 [−0.02197, −0.01231], AUC 0.604).

The effect is **not specific to zinc fingers**, and saying so is part of the claim. Among 93 HGNC families with ≥ 30 held genes, zinc fingers rank 13th–21st; homeoboxes (AUC 0.603) and bHLH factors (0.574) show the same deficit. The axis runs from core machinery — spliceosome, proteasome, ribosome biogenesis, +0.07 … +0.11 above the rest — to cell-identity families: receptors, homeoboxes, zinc fingers.

Is it biology or measurement? The deficit **grows with the reliability tercile** (AUC 0.517 → 0.551 → 0.614) and 74 % of it survives an explicit reliability correction — the opposite of what a measurement artefact would do (the tercile contrasts are not expression-matched; a stated limit). An independent estimator corroborates it: inside the CD4 source's own K562-vs-CD4 table (DESeq2 z-scores, 56 zinc fingers) the deficit reads Δmedian −0.030 / −0.032 / −0.032, AUC 0.58–0.59, one-sided P 0.013–0.019 — **but the bootstrap interval clears zero in only one of the three culture conditions**. Corroboration, not confirmation, and reported as such.

The direction is what the lineage-dependency and co-essentiality literature expects; the family-resolved, preregistered, magnitude- and reliability-controlled quantification is the new part, which is why the audit graded it EXTENDED rather than NEW.

**F12 as amended by 284 — conservation is strongly predictable at the family level and only partly at the gene level, but the gene-level remainder is real. Provenance: EXTENDED (both F12a and F12b). Confirmation: preregistered (281 hash 08:15:18Z; the 284 rerun hash 13:46:19Z, with 281's primary cell reproduced at 0.0 × 10⁰ first); no ledger claim — this is held-gene, not reserved, data.**

From features known before any perturbation is measured (DepMap essentiality and gene effect, CCLE expression breadth and level, regulon size, TF class, complex membership, paralogue count, HGNC family — 21 features), a ridge predicts 279's 17,106-gene conservation map with families held out.

The first run (281) blocked its folds on each gene's *first* HGNC group, so a multi-family gene could share another family across the split; the rerun (284) excluded **every** membership of a held family. The leak was real — 43.7 % of 281's test genes shared a family with a training gene — and it did **not** inflate the result: on the all-membership split, ridge **ρ +0.334 [+0.216, +0.355]**, R² 0.112, against 281's +0.320 [+0.256, +0.379].

At the family level the axis is strongly predictable. Under folds in which a test gene shares **no** family with any training row, out-of-fold predicted family medians reproduce the observed axis at **ρ +0.805 [+0.679, +0.883]** over **78 families** (Figure F-4a). The zinc-finger deficit is **57 % predicted** from biology alone (−0.01009 of an observed −0.01785), against 84 % on the leaky first-family folds and 80 % when the same strict-fold model is read on first-family blocks — the drop mixes the leak with a smaller training set (6,289 non-giant genes), and we release the conservative number.

At the gene level, annotation adds nothing over measured reliability: features minus reliability reads **−0.035 [−0.082, +0.033]**, and on the measurement-matched core the features add **+0.015 [−0.009, +0.029]** on top of reliability (reliability ρ +0.529 vs features +0.413).

**The claim that was withdrawn, and what replaced it.** F12's original reading — "the gene-level residual is mostly measurement" — is **WITHDRAWN**. 284 tested it directly. Split the 28 sources into two halves sharing no source and no cell line; compute each half's gene-level remainder (its own map minus the annotation model's out-of-fold prediction); correlate the two remainders. They agree at **ρ +0.328 [+0.241, +0.373]** against a ceiling — the same correlation between the two halves' *raw* maps — of **+0.382 [+0.267, +0.410]**, a within-component permutation null of +0.044 ± 0.018 with 0 of 200 draws reaching the observed value, and **20 of 20 admissible partitions positive** (median +0.319; Figure F-4b). It survives removing measured reliability and response magnitude as well (**+0.277 [+0.175, +0.304]**) and reads +0.322 on the 1,816 genes off the response axis, whose own knockdown cannot contribute to the statistic.

So per-gene conservation biology exists beyond the family axis, and the 21 annotation features do not carry it. The described tail is interpretable: mRNA-surveillance and exon-junction machinery (SMG5, SMG7, UPF2, CASC3, ZC3H13, EIF3H) is conserved *more* than essentiality predicts, and proteostasis and chromosome-segregation machinery (CDC37, VCP, PSMB4, NCAPG, NCAPD2, HAUS6) *less* — the remainder separates **within** the essential genes, where the essentiality × expression axis has no resolution. Two caveats belong in the same sentence: both halves share the 28 banks' pipelines and the same 2,739-gene axis, so a shared processing artefact would appear in both, and "gene property" is a statement about reproducibility, not mechanism.

### 3(d) Response geometry

**F13 — a response's cross-line conservation is predictable from its own geometry measured in other lines, with the preregistered mechanism inverted. ### 3(e) State dependence at the granularity where it exists

**F5 — on Broad-16 a reproducible line-specific response component exists, is high-rank, and is not predictable transcriptome-wide by a linear model from basal expression or from the source paper's own state features. ### 3(f) Why a rank-retrieval metric rewards noise directions

**F3 — the whitening operator has learnt its training rows' noise, and retrieval needs those directions. Provenance: F3a EXTENDED (the eigenvalue-bias half is textbook and cited as such); F3b NEW. Confirmation: none required — F3a is a property of fitted objects on disk; F3b is measured on three PLOLO folds and re-read on the Flex reference.**

The pipeline whitens donor rows through an operator fitted on those donors at n/p ≈ 1.6. In the fitted eigenbasis, with the noise covariance estimated from the donors' own batch half-differences, an independent noise estimate **exceeds the fitted eigenvalue on 76.9 / 77.5 / 78.3 %** of the directions of the three fold operators and **78.7 %** of the production operator. Those directions carry **87.5 / 87.7 / 87.9 / 91.5 %** of the whitened prediction's energy, and Σnoise / Σeigenvalue is 0.88–1.00: the covariance the operator was fitted on is about 90 % sampling noise (Figure F-6a). This is the Marchenko–Pastur signature of eigenvectors chosen to minimise their own rows' noise, and it is textbook ([LEDOIT04], [GAVISH14], [RMT-SC]); we cite it as such.

The consequence is visible on the same rows: whitened through the operator fitted **on them**, a donor's two batch halves are *anti*-correlated (median −0.13 … −0.24, 97–100 % ≤ 0) while the held line's halves, out-of-sample through the same operator, read **+0.03 … +0.07**; a properly cross-fitted operator restores +0.007 … +0.022.

**F3b is the new part, and it runs against the standard prescription** — with one citation that must travel with it: Jolliffe (1982) already showed that low-variance principal components can be as important as high-variance ones in prediction [JOLLIFFE82], so the surprise is relative to the whitening literature's classification prior, not to statistics. The embedding-retrieval and covariance literature recommends shrinkage-regularised whitening ([WHITEN-CAVEAT], [LEDOIT04]). Applying it here *loses* rank retrieval (Figure F-6b): Wiener damping at κ = 1, which keeps 7 % of the whitened energy, costs **−0.034 [−0.044, −0.024]** in `pds` on 3 of 3 PLOLO folds; a hard cut of the noise-dominated directions costs **−0.069 [−0.085, −0.054]**; both beat their shuffled controls, so the loss is discrimination, not amplitude. The soft floor c = 1, which keeps 68 %, costs **−0.0022 [−0.0040, −0.0004]**. On the reliable Flex-400 reference the same arms give −0.013 [−0.035, +0.012] and **−0.0001 [−0.0053, +0.0043]** — the same sign, so the loss is the prediction's transfer, not the truth's noise.

Why: the target-specific signal is only **5–10 %** of a donor row's energy (norm moments 0.09 / 0.07 / 0.07; the half-difference identity 0.105 / 0.054 / 0.064) and it is spread over exactly the low-variance directions the operator amplifies. Damping them removes the discrimination before it removes the noise.

**The obvious objection — that the eigenvectors themselves are contaminated — was tested and does not rescue the prescription.** 286 estimated the signal covariance Ŝ and noise covariance N̂ from **cross-products of independent batch halves** over every bank row except the panel (9,500–17,700 rows; the moment identity Ĉ = Ŝ + 2N̂ holds to 5 × 10⁻¹⁷). The object is real and different: shuffling the pairing shrinks Ŝ's leading eigenvalue by 7–19×, and Ŝ's top-100 directions sit at principal cosines 0.52–0.70 to the fitted operator's. Used as a full-matrix Wiener filter, a low-rank projection, or the whitener's covariance, it **does not clear anywhere**: 0 of 12 cells, best −0.0080 [−0.0163, +0.0001] on 1 of 3 folds, with low-rank truncation catastrophic (−0.14 … −0.18). And the mechanism survives: **78.1–79.8 % of the directions have λ⁺(Ŝ) below the independent noise level and carry 80.8–81.8 % of the incumbent's whitened prediction energy**, while Ŝ's top-100 carry 1.0–1.1 %. Only 4–11 % of a half row's covariance is shared response, and its top-100 eigen-directions hold 19–32 % of that — the shared response is itself high-rank, which is why truncation destroys retrieval. A third route to the same conclusion (299, 300): teaching the whitener preferentially from the training rows whose independent halves agree loses retrieval on every fold, and a dose curve at fixed row count is monotone with the opposite sign — the rows that reproduce least are the ones the metric wants in the covariance. Spread over the leading directions is part of what that reliability axis measures (a direct low-leverage up-weighting is 72 % collinear per target) but not the operative part (it delivers a quarter of the effect): what the metric pays for lives in the tail of the spectrum, where a leading-direction leverage rank is blind. The effect is real — three controls pass — and small: its entire realisable span is +0.0015 in `pds`, a third of the scorer's ordering noise.

**F4 — the metric's threshold, and what it means. Provenance: F4a EXTENDED with a caveat that must travel with it; F4b EXTENDED.**

Per-target `pds` is a step function of the prediction's own cosine to its own truth (Figure F-8): mean `pds` by bin runs 0.15 / 0.37 / 0.66 / 0.86 / 0.97 / 0.99 / 1.00 on PLOLO (n = 853, 290 decoys) and 0.16 / 0.45 / 0.76 / 0.92 / 0.98 / 1.00 on Flex-400 (n = 85, 85 decoys), both crossing 0.97 in the (0.02, 0.03] bin. **The caveat is not optional**: a rank-among-decoys statistic saturates as soon as the own cosine clears the upper tail of the decoy-cosine distribution, so the knee is largely *derivable* from that distribution and its location moves with the decoy count — the slope inside (0, 0.05] is 9.5 at 290 decoys and 6.0 at 85. The content is the empirical decoy distribution, not the existence of a knee, and it is not a discovery. The metric's geometry is itself an active literature ([PDSGEOM], [CDS26], [PERTURBENCH]).

The decomposition of where a transfer prediction fails is a measurement with no prior report. On PLOLO, **17.5 %** of panel targets are anti-aligned (the base prediction points away from the truth), **66 %** sit in the marginal band (0, 0.05] and **16 %** are saturated; the two former groups carry **53.1 %** and **46.8 %** of the deficit. On Flex-400 the shares are 12.9 / 48 / 39 %. Per-target `pds` is essentially a threshold in one covariate: ρ(base·truth cosine, per-target `pds`) = **+0.875**. The failure is fold-consistent (bottom-decile overlap 9 / 12 / 12 against an expected 2.2–2.5; Spearman across held lines +0.76 / +0.70 / +0.74) — the same targets fail in different recipient lines, which is what [TRANSPORT26] predicts when causal mechanisms differ across contexts. Scope: an observed 0.02 means a different *signal* cosine on each reference (≈ 0.07–0.09 on PLOLO's 0.05-reliable truths, ≈ 0.03 on Flex), so the two marginal bands are different target sets.

### 3(g) What a construct does besides its intended gene

**F16 — in the challenge's own 2025 CRISPRi library, the genes a construct's protospacer seed matches in their promoters are more repressed in that construct's own cells, in aggregate, and, as the seed off-target literature has held since Jackson *et al.* 2003 [JACKSON03] and as Rohatgi *et al.* 2024 report for CRISPRi [ROHATGI24], the effect follows the protospacer rather than the intended target. Audited by 320: **KNOWN 4, EXTENDED 1, NEW 0.** The phenomenon ([ROHATGI24]; [HARTMAN26]), the seed-plus-promoter rule and its measured predictive precision, the partner-guide specificity control ([JACKSON03]; [HARTMAN26]) and the aggregate-over-per-item statistical logic are all published. What is ours is the design — construct as the unit, with a joint null over candidate sets matched on a measured library-wide off-target propensity — and a re-measurement of the phenomenon on the challenge's own library, whose specificity its producers did not assess. Confirmation: ledger claim C8 (preregistered 2026-09-07 16:00:01Z; verdict `UNRESOLVABLE-AT-THIS-RESOLUTION` — it aborted itself) and claim C9 (preregistered 16:27:26Z; verdict **PASS**).**

*The provenance, item by item (320).* Nothing biological in §3(g) survives as new; the one **EXTENDED** column is the design, and it is a recombination of two published methods rather than a new one.

| item | verdict | the prior report it repeats or extends |

*The question and the unit.* §3(a) established that a construct which misses its gene still delivers a reproducible transcriptome, and left open what that transcriptome is (T7). One candidate explanation is an off-target of the specific spacer. The test asks one aggregate question over **all 34** gene-targeting constructs of the 2025 library, covering **17 paired intended targets**: are the genes a construct's own seed rule selects more repressed in the cells carrying that construct than sequence-, promoter- and expression-matched genes the same rule did not select? The unit resampled is the **construct**, not the construct–gene pair — 83 construct–gene pairs over 34 constructs. Candidates come from a seed rule frozen by an earlier experiment (301) and reproduced here by a known-answer row — the longest seed length in {12, 11, 10, 9} that yields a hit in the CRISPRi active window after a 10 kb distal filter — which gives L\* = 12 for 8 constructs, 11 for 16, 10 for 7 and 9 for 3; every one of the 34 has a non-empty set, so no construct is dropped for lacking a strong candidate.

*The null is the design, and it is what makes the test able to fail.* Genes are not resampled independently; **candidate sets are**. Each real candidate is replaced by a gene from its own matched pool, matched jointly on the *measured* probability that a real library protospacer hits that gene off-target under the same rule — obtained by rescanning all **209,070** hCRISPRi-v2 protospacers, not modelled — and on promoter GC, TSS count and density, a mappability proxy and basal control expression, with every gene the library targets, the construct's own target and every gene its own spacers touch excluded. With **B = 100,000** joint draws the smallest attainable p is **1.0 × 10⁻⁵**, three orders of magnitude below α = 0.05. This rate is not comparable with published seed-match rates: our per-spacer L = 12 promoter-hit probability is **0.132353** for the 68 real 2025 spacers and **0.160879** for the pool, against the 47.2–59.8 % of guides [HARTMAN26] report with a ≥ 12 bp match within 1 kb of a TSS, because our rule is confined to the CRISPRi active window (TSS offset −50 … +300) and applies a 10 kb distal filter. Ours is the stricter rule, by design. That floor is the design difference from the per-pair test this replaced (305), whose same-split rank statistic had a Holm floor of 11/158 ≈ **0.0696** at its declared family of eleven — **0.0506** on the eight pairs that turned out testable, at the train split's empirical rank-p floor of 0.0063 — and which therefore could not reject *for any effect however large*. Here a negative would have been informative.

*C8: both pass conditions met, and the experiment aborted itself anyway.* The estimate was **S = +0.786** standardised units per construct, construct-level bootstrap 95 % **[+0.387, +1.166]** (n = 34), joint-null p = 9.9999 × 10⁻⁶ at the floor, maximum leave-one-construct-out p = 1.99998 × 10⁻⁵; **Δ = −0.0790 log1p-CP50k [−0.1366, −0.0310]**, about **7.6 %** below matched genes. But 2 of 7 preregistered known-answer rows failed: the no-signal row measured a construct-independent bias of **+0.0683** against a ≤ 0.050 rule (**+0.0717 ± 0.0022** at 5,000 derangements — 9 % of the observed effect), and the matching-balance row missed on one axis of nine, promoter-window count at SMD **+0.2905** against ≤ 0.250. The frozen abort clause governs a known-answer failure whatever the primary says, and it was honoured rather than loosened: C8 is reported as a bounded estimate with an interval, not a verdict.

*C9: the two defects repaired, the identical hypothesis re-tested at the identical α.* Neither defect is a defect of the hypothesis; both are defects of the null and the matching. The repair centres every construct's score on **its own wrong-recipient distribution** — the same candidate genes, in the same matched pools, read in the cells of every other targeting construct of the same split (15 / 9 / 7 recipients) — and adds promoter-window count to the hard strata (10 basal deciles × 5 propensity quintiles × 3 window classes = 150 cells). No threshold was loosened and the verdict cell, the statistic family and α = 0.05 one-sided are unchanged. The result is **S = +0.71293 [+0.32419, +1.07351]**, joint-null **p = 1.99998 × 10⁻⁵** (attainable floor 1.0 × 10⁻⁵), maximum leave-one-construct-out p = **8.99991 × 10⁻⁵**, **Δ = −0.078205 [−0.135970, −0.030017]**, and **nine of nine** known-answer rows pass. 26 of 34 constructs are positive (exact binomial p = 0.00147).

*What that p resamples, and what it does not (320).* That p is a **gene-sampling** p — the resampled unit is the candidate gene, not the construct — and its three-orders-below-α resolution is a property of the 100,000 draws, not of the 34 units. In [GOEMAN07]'s terms it is a *competitive, gene-sampling* null, and such nulls are anti-conservative under residual dependence between the resampled items; the covariate matching removes the confounders it measures, but it does not make genes exchangeable. The construct-level readings are the construct bootstrap interval **[+0.32419, +1.07351]**, the paired partner-construct Wilcoxon **p = 0.00459**, and, in C8 only, the wrong-recipient construct permutation at its floor **p = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴**; under C9's per-construct centring that permutation is the same object as KA-4R and is no longer independent, so C9's construct-level inference rests on the bootstrap interval and the partner Wilcoxon. Those, not 2.0 × 10⁻⁵, are the numbers a construct-level null supports. This weakens nothing about the conclusion — the interval excludes zero and the paired test is significant — but it corrects which number carries the weight, and the phrase "the construct as the experimental unit" describes the *estimand and the interval*, not the headline p.

*The repair that mattered works against the effect, and was declared to do so before it was run.* Decomposed, the matching repair moves the estimate by **+0.0023** (+0.3 %) and the bias repair by **−0.0750** — **−9.5 %**, from +0.78797 on the repaired pools to +0.71293 — so the whole of C9's movement relative to C8 is a subtraction. It did not narrow the null (repaired sd 0.17316 against 0.17173), which is why p rose from the floor 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ to 2.0 × 10⁻⁵. Per-construct centring rather than a single scalar was chosen for a reason that the data then confirmed: the wrong-recipient level has mean +0.07504 but standard deviation **0.42538** across constructs, a spread **5.7×** the mean it scatters around. The two variants have the same point estimate by linearity, so the choice could not have been made for its effect on the answer; it changes only the interval.

*The partner-construct test is the design's load-bearing element — and it is not ours.* Reading the same candidate genes in the cells of a different guide against the same target is the standard specificity control of the seed off-target field: it is [JACKSON03]'s founding observation that different siRNAs against one transcript give *siRNA-specific rather than target-specific* signatures, and it is used on CRISPRi Perturb-seq for this exact purpose by [HARTMAN26] and as the discovery device by [ROHATGI24]. What is ours is only its systematic paired application to all 34 constructs. Each construct's candidate genes were re-scored in the cells of its **partner construct — same intended target, same split, same controls, different protospacers**. The identical genes read at **+0.04296 [−0.29799, +0.38107]** there against **+0.78797** in their own construct's cells; own − partner **+0.74500 [+0.22896, +1.25766]**, paired Wilcoxon p = **0.00459**, n = 34, with all 34 partners inside the same split. This excludes intended-target downstream biology and generic candidate-gene properties, and leaves the association attached to the protospacer sequence. A structurally different null agrees: resampling *sequences* rather than genes — a pseudo-construct is a random pair of stringency-0 library protospacers rescanned through the same rule — gives **+0.67242, p = 0.00149** at its own floor.

*Three facts that sit awkwardly with a simple seed-binding reading, reported as such.* First, the effect is **not concentrated at the longest seed**: by level it reads +0.685 (L\* = 12, n = 8), **+0.963** (L\* = 11, n = 16), +0.197 (L\* = 10, n = 7) and +0.658 (L\* = 9, n = 3). A strictly seed-length-graded mechanism predicts the opposite ordering. Second, there is still **no failing-versus-working contrast** — +0.8333 against +0.6384, difference +0.1949 [−0.5584, +0.9434], Mann–Whitney p = 0.7231, with the sign opposite to C8's −0.077 [−0.869, +0.699] and both intervals several times the difference. That axis is unresolved at n = 34, not positive. Third, the 31 **non-targeting** constructs read **+0.43522 [+0.03554, +0.82811]**, p = 0.00666, about 61 % of the targeting aggregate. These are not negative controls — a non-targeting construct still carries two protospacers that can seed-match promoters and its candidates come from the identical rule — so a positive there is what a sequence-carried explanation predicts, on constructs where intended-target biology cannot be the cause at all. It is corroborating, and it is not a verdict cell.

*The multiplicity, stated plainly.* **C8 and C9 are two tests of one hypothesis on one data set** — the same 34 constructs, the same cells, the same sums, the same candidate genes. **This is not an independent replication and nothing here should be read as one.** Holm or Bonferroni over the two attempts requires the smaller p ≤ 0.025, which 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ satisfies arithmetically, but the arithmetic is not the interesting part: the real multiplicity is *analytic*, because C9's design was chosen after seeing C8's diagnostics and the repaired estimate was predictable in advance from C8's own published post-hoc bias correction (+0.71399). What C9 buys is that the estimate is not an artefact of the two calibration defects C8 found. It does not add data, does not narrow the interval, does not make one experiment into two, and does not upgrade an association into a mechanism. One check was also *lost* in the repair and we say so: the wrong-recipient permutation, an independent reading in C8, is under per-construct centring the same object as the repaired no-signal row, which is why the sequence-resampling null carries more of the corroboration burden in C9 than it did in C8. C9's audit of C8 also found a split-size misstatement in C8's write-up (16 / 10 / 8, not the 21 / 7 / 6 its prose reports); the table C8's code actually read carries 16 / 10 / 8, so no C8 number is affected.

*What this does not establish — in the body, not only in the limitations.* **It does not establish dCas9-KRAB off-target binding.** Expression here is observational and nothing measured is an occupancy assay. The 34 constructs differ in on-target knockdown (−3.04 … +0.03 in log-fold change), in cell number (95 … 3,415), in population composition and in handling; **no construct has a biological replicate**; and **all three splits share one control pool** — 38,176 control cells that are the same cells in all three files, so the three splits are not three independent experiments for any control-referenced statistic (the primary is a within-construct rank, which is why a shift common to a construct's genes cancels). The partner test narrows the explanation to **something carried by the protospacer**, and it **cannot separate seed-mediated binding from a construct artefact correlated with spacer identity**. It is not a population off-target rate: 83 candidates over 34 constructs and 17 intended targets cannot support one. And with the construct as the unit **the interval is set by n = 34** — more cells per construct do not narrow it; more constructs would. Two further robustness readings are worth having in the same paragraph: 33 of the 83 candidates sit below the measurement floor used earlier in the programme and the estimate on above-floor candidates only is **+0.65229**, so this is not a low-expression artefact; and the one element of the repair whose direction favours the effect — a global exclusion needed to make the pools recipient-independent — is worth **+0.00034**, 0.05 % of the estimate.

*What a new experiment would add, and the earlier recommendation withdrawn.* An earlier version of this section proposed a **sequence-mutant control** — re-cloning the candidate-bearing constructs with silent seed mutations, about 24 constructs × ~500 cells, order $3–6 k — as *the decisive next experiment*, and a dCas9 occupancy assay at $5–10 k beside it. **That recommendation is withdrawn as written.** The mechanism it was meant to establish is not open: [ROHATGI24] already demonstrates seed-mediated off-target activity at the level of **binding**, with dCas9 ChIP-seq on four seed-off-target guides (98–1,141 reproducible peaks each, 10–33 % promoter-proximal, 12 bp of seed complementarity sometimes crucial for strong occupancy), in this library family, two years before this measurement. An occupancy assay here would add construct-specific confirmation, not the mechanism. The sequence-mutant control remains the only design that separates seed-mediated binding from a construct artefact travelling with spacer identity **for these particular constructs**, so what it buys is local attribution on a 34-construct sample of a phenomenon already characterised — which is little, and we say so rather than price it. The cheap route, if it ever exists, is an independent screen carrying the same library: it would allow the identical statistic with cell line as a second unit at no cost, and §3(h) reports that none does. What is certain is that no further analysis of these cells settles it.

*A defect in a published resource, found on the way and worth recording.* The propensity model above required rescanning the parent library's supplementary table ([HORLBECK16]). Its gene labels are Excel-escaped as `="SYMBOL"`, which silently stopped an own-target exclusion from firing and inflated a per-spacer hit rate at the 12-nt seed from **0.107 to 0.597**. It was caught by the real-versus-pool spacer calibration check before the preregistration was hashed, and the corrected pool is certified by a known-answer row in the second experiment: **0** escaped labels of 209,070 rows, pool hit rate **0.160879** and real-spacer rate **0.132353** reproducing at a difference of 0.0 × 10⁰, with the bug-equivalent rate recorded at 0.597. Anyone using that table's gene symbols to exclude a spacer's own target should check for the escape.

### 3(h) What bounds zero-shot prediction in this challenge

Collecting the ceilings measured above gives a quantitative statement of the task's difficulty that does not depend on any particular model.

**The construct ceiling is real, quantified, and not what is binding today.** A truth in this challenge is 400 cells of **one** construct, and 293 measures the ceiling that fact imposes in the challenge's own regime. Under a variance model in which a construct's true response is a gene-level part plus a construct-specific part plus 400-cell sampling noise, a *perfect construct-level* prediction would score cos **+0.690 [+0.510, +0.847]** against the scored truth, and a *perfect gene-level* prediction — the best any zero-shot entry can do, since no 2026 construct exists on disk and no model is told which vector produced the truth — would score cos **+0.213 [+0.000, +0.348]**; matched on knockdown, the regime ~83 % of the library is in, the gene-level ceiling rises to ≈ **+0.572**. The honest bracket is therefore **cos ≈ 0.21 … 0.57 for a zero-shot model against 0.69 for one that knew the construct**. Because the 2026 panel shares only **25 of 300** targets with the 2025 H1 library and no source on disk carries the 2026 constructs, that gap is a floor under every entry, not a disadvantage of one method.

**It is not, however, the current constraint,** and the earlier reading of this fact inside the programme was too strong. The incumbent pipeline's median per-target transfer cosine on the same Flex reference is ≈ **0.04**, about five times *below* even the pessimistic 0.21. The construct term is a large ceiling with a great deal of room beneath it.

**A back-of-envelope this supersedes.** F14 raised, as a hypothesis, that a cross-construct cross-line prediction can share only ρ_guide × ρ_line of a truth's reproducible variance — a few per cent with ρ_guide ≈ 0.09–0.41 and ρ_line ≈ 0.35–0.45 — and noted that this is "of the order of" the incumbent's median own cosine of 0.037. That comparison conflated a *variance share* with a *cosine*, and 293's direct measurement replaces it: the gene-level cosine ceiling is 0.21–0.57, not 0.04. We record the earlier reading here because it circulated, and because the arithmetic error it contains is easy to repeat.

**Against the metric.** F4 says a prediction's rank score is decided at an own cosine of about 0.02, with 48–66 % of targets in the marginal band on either reference. The lever is therefore per-target transfer direction on that band.

**What has been measured not to move it.** Per-target selection of a donor row by proximity to the truth (in-sample +0.056, cross-validated **−0.018 [−0.027, −0.009]**, retained fraction −0.32); a leakage-safe utility router (+0.0001 [−0.0024, +0.0028]); reliability weighting and empirical-Bayes shrinkage of donor rows; noise-corrected re-weighting of the fitted eigenbasis (F3b); a signal covariance from independent views (286); fitting the whitener on reliability-selected training rows (299: −0.067 in `pds` on 3 of 3 folds, below matched random, magnitude-selected and reversed controls, a row-count effect below full rank and null at full rank — while a rank-preserving tilt of the training rows *away* from the reproducible ones gains +0.0009 [+0.0002, +0.0017] on 3 of 3 folds with mirror and random-weight controls passing — and, dosed (300), is monotone without saturating, so that the dose that would pay for the shape-only gate always leaves the shape-only band: no dose clears, and the whole effect is a third of the scorer's ordering-noise standard deviation); class-conditional rendering keyed on the conservation map; and weighting each donor row by that donor's own measured on-target knockdown (308). Each closes **its own construction**, not the field (rule 6). What remains is an oracle ceiling of **+0.046 [+0.038, +0.054]** for a drop-one operation that requires knowing the held line — every build-time arbiter tested agrees with it at 44–61 % against a 66–70 % break-even.

**The last of those closes with a mechanism rather than a null, which is why it is worth stating in full.** §3(a) and §4.3 make guide efficacy the dominant term of a perturbation response, so the obvious competition-facing corollary is that a donor screen's own on-target knockdown |kd| should identify which donor's row to trust. The premise survives inspection: |kd| is genuinely new information — build-time observable, read from one donor bank and nothing else, defined for **226 / 290 / 294 of the 300 panel targets** in the three donor screens, **absent from the incumbent's base by construction**, nearly orthogonal to the row norm (Spearman of the log |kd| ratio with the log norm ratio **+0.03 / +0.03 / −0.005**) and only weakly a reliability proxy (**+0.04 … +0.19**) — and it acts on a large set, **106 of 300** panel targets where the two donors disagree, so what follows is not a power problem. It still fails. Zero of 16 arms clear over 51 screened cells with 64 of 64 known-answer rows passing; the hard gate at the relative cut loses (−0.00863 [−0.01413, −0.00335], 0 of 3 folds) and the soft weight loses monotonically in its dose parameter on both cuts; the one cell positive on 3 of 3 folds reads `pds` **+0.00077 [−0.00114, +0.00283]**, prices at **−0.00145** pessimistic against a +0.0089 gate, and is **indistinguishable from entropy-matched random re-weighting of the same rows** (two of three random draws larger than the arm). The measured reason is the useful part: **on the set where the donors disagree, the higher-|kd| donor's row is not the one closer to the held truth** — dropping the apparently failing donor moves more targets *down* than up in all six (cut × fold) cells by **1.5–2.4×** (on the relative cut with HEK293T held, 25 up against 53 down, mean Δ`pds` −0.031). **The construct term is a ceiling on the truths; it is not a usable selector among donors.** With this, per-target donor re-weighting on the three production donors is closed on reliability, agreement, norm, arbiter, spread *and* measured on-target knockdown, and the equal-weight mean has survived every key tried.

**What would move it.** In order of measured value: (i) **knockdown-validated constructs, and a second construct per target**, in any new data — worth more than more cells of the first, since a construct that misses its gene still delivers a reproducible transcriptome and cells stop helping at 76–330 per side; (ii) **the challenge's own library** in a public screen, the only thing that could carry the construct-specific component; (iii) depth, but only to 76–330 cells per side. Breadth — more cell lines at 150 cells — adds little over two donors on this panel (Δρ +0.066 [+0.004, +0.133] on one of three readings). And a property of the benchmark rather than of any model: at least some failing constructs deliver a reproducible response to the vector rather than to the gene (two documented in 293), which caps every entry equally. The rate is bounded from the libraries' own calls rather than extrapolated: **roughly one target in five to one in four** carries a truth whose construct did not knock the gene down (18.7 % of Arc's validated 2025 panel by |lfc| < 1.0; 26.7 % of 73,765 guide × condition rows by the CD4 authors' own significance call), and 296 shows such a construct's response is reproducible in all five libraries while carrying no shared vector signature. What that response is — an off-target of the specific spacer (the characterised seed mechanism covers 0.8–4.7 % of guides [HARTMAN26]), a sub-threshold knockdown, or a transduced-population effect — is still not distinguished for any individual construct, though §3(g) now measures an aggregate association with the spacer sequence across all 34 constructs of the 2025 library (T7).

### 3(i) What an L1 error on significance-selected genes rewards

**F17 — An L1 fold-change error scored only on the reference's significant genes has a closed-form optimum under sign
uncertainty. It sits near the minimum detectable effect, and it can be bought without better biology. Provenance: EXTENDED. The
statistics are textbook (the L1 minimiser is a median; selection on significance truncates |l| from below); the
application to a perturbation-prediction metric, the calibration on real data and the delivery are new. Confirmation: H1 (5 seeds) and the preregistered board read of 2026-09-24 (C-58).**

*The metric.* `de_wilcoxon_lfc_nmae` scores, per perturbation, Σ|l̂ − l| / Σ|l| over the genes the reference calls
significant (Wilcoxon, BH 0.05, control mean > 5 CPM), with l the log2 ratio of per-cell-normalised arithmetic means.

*The optimum.* Take a gene whose predicted sign is right with probability a, and write F for the distribution of |l|
given significance.
- For a predicted magnitude m, the expected error changes at rate 1 − 2a·P(|l| > m).
- So the posterior median, and the L1-optimal m, is **m\* = F⁻¹(1 − 1/(2a))**.
- Below |l|, each unit of magnitude buys (2a − 1).
- Significance bounds |l| from below by the minimum detectable effect. For 400 perturbed against 18,400 control cells
  under Poisson counting, that is MDE ≈ 0.24/√(control count per cell).

*Calibration on H1.*
- On h1-test's gates (58 targets, 64,931 gene cells), the 10th percentile of |l| tracks 0.24/√count across the
  expression range.
- The pipeline's delivered magnitudes were ≈ 0.17× the truth, far below the MDE.
- Adding sign · min(c/√count, cap) to every steered gene wins an exponent scan at exactly ½ (the Poisson law).
- It is worth nmae **−0.0096 ± 0.0002** at c = cap = 0.1 (5 seeds), with the other five members flat.

*Delivery.* The add must not move the pseudobulk (the two direction members) or the Wilcoxon ranks (the three
call-set members).
- It is written into the per-cell class split of each gene's fixed total.
- The emitter's library classes make that a channel of about ±0.5 log2.
- Two constraints bind:
  - **class capacity.** A fine-step polish drained the middle library class, and in the line with the least slack (A)
    the plain delivery reached zero dose. Moving whole counts between the extreme classes only makes an A-balanced
    set of moves count-balanced, and restores the dose (0 → 0.96).
  - **call leakage.** Doses above c = 0.1 create hundreds to thousands of calls the design cannot suppress.

*What the optimum implies about everyone else.* Flip the true gate signs at rate 1 − a and give each gene its
L1-optimal magnitude. H1's nmae then maps to sign accuracy: 0.95 at a = 0.56 (this pipeline, measured), 0.88 at 0.62,
and 0.84 at 0.65.
- The public board's leaders sat at 0.82–0.84 when this section was written (2026-09-24), the 0.65 point of that curve (0.56 here); on 09-29 the leader posted 0.804 (§4.7). The curve assumes iid signs and
  constant magnitudes: if magnitudes track confidence, the same nmae is reached at a lower average accuracy, so 0.65 is an
  upper bound on what those entries need, not a measurement of their signs.
- 71 % of the gate's |l| mass lies in genes this pipeline does not call, where its sign accuracy is 0.537.
- A fitted conditional-median model over our own statistics and the panel's donor tables adds nothing beyond the
  1/√count law (434).
- The remaining gap is direction information, not magnitude.

*Why this belongs in a paper about what references measure.*
- A benchmark member that reads fold changes only where the reference is significant rewards a calibrated magnitude
  floor, even from a predictor whose signs are barely better than chance.
- Its scaled value therefore mixes two quantities that should be reported separately:
  - sign information, the biology;
  - magnitude calibration against the selection threshold, an artefact of the selection.
- We recommend that benchmark designers report the sign-accuracy-equivalent alongside the scaled score, using the
  mapping above, which needs only the reference.

- On the genes the design does not call, the hook replaces the design's own delivered sign. That sign is right 0.532 of the
  time on H1's uncalled gate genes, where the programme is right 0.685 (P_down, τ 0.05; 0.702 for the mean-z estimate). A
  block prior must instead beat the cross-line consensus on the high-expression block.
- A weaker programme can be emulated by flipping a random fraction r of its signs. On nmae, the member it moves, the hook
  beats the consensus hook until r ≈ 0.42 (mean z) … 0.38 (P_down). Over all six members it stays positive to r ≈ 0.28 …
  0.37, against r ≈ 0.17 (every target) … 0.24 (design-strong targets) for the block prior. The six-member reading carries
  a small-gate reach term from r 0.2 that H1 cannot price for sign priors (Figure F-13).
- The estimator (mean z over non-essential rows rather than P_down) was chosen on these instruments and on the cross-line
  analogue of §3(j), so its H1 price is in-sample.
- The board test is preregistered: arm 446bj2zmz, primary Δnmae raw vs 446bj2z; ≤ −0.0030 transfers, −0.0030 … −0.0008
  partial, > −0.0008 fails; pds and mse must be bit-identical. It has not been read.


### 3(j) What a transfer model's DE signs miss, and the one kind of data that supplies it

**F18 — The sign gap between a zero-shot transfer model and an oracle on a perturbation's significant genes is the
recipient line's own shared response programme. It is unobservable from the recipient's unperturbed cells and absent
from other lines. A screen of the same line from a different lab and chemistry carried it on H1, together with most of the
target-specific response. The one scored test on a challenge line (Jurkat) read null for the programme. Among cancer
lines the per-gene programme is mostly a lab and protocol signature. On matched perturbation sets, different lines within a
lab agree at r 0.54 and the same line across labs at 0.21, while the target-specific response replicates across labs. Provenance: PENDING AUDIT. Likely KNOWN in part: a common response shared by many
perturbations (Replogle 2022); the same component named "systematic variation" and quantified across ten datasets and
five lines as a confounder of evaluation (Systema, Viñas Torné et al. 2025, Nat Biotechnol); within-line agreement
between K562 screens; line-specific drug responses with a shared
component across pooled cancer lines (MIX-Seq, McFarland 2020); a template along the control identity axis (the July 2026
decomposition preprint). Candidate NEW: that the programme is a
transferable property of the line rather than only a confounder, its line-specificity at per-gene sign resolution, the negatives for control-cell and cross-line routes, and the size of the
same-line effect through a benchmark's own scorer. Sources: experiments 440, 441, 450, 462, 466, 467, 516, 517. The same laboratory's programme from another line is tested on the challenge's own lines by the 09-30 entries: [[PENDING 09-30]].**

*The measurement.* On the H1 reference (h1-test: 85 targets, the scorer's own Wilcoxon table), compare the sign of each
target's significant genes with four predictors: our cross-line transfer model; a leave-one-target-out mean of the
other targets' reference responses (the recipient's own programme, an oracle); a cross-line consensus of four other
lines' mean responses (CLEAN4); and features of the recipient's control cells.
- Where the transfer model calls a gene, its signs match or beat the oracle programme (0.649 vs 0.640, |l|-weighted).
- On the ~60 % of significant genes it does not call, it is near chance (0.54) and the oracle reaches 0.63.
- Other lines' mean responses reach 0.57–0.58. Restricting a donor line's mean to the recipient's own panel makes it
  worse. Control-cell principal components, a G1 axis and a cell-size axis all read 0.51–0.56.
- The control identity axis (genes higher in this line than in others tend to go down) is real but explains ~6 % of the
  programme.

*The same line from another lab.* Pan et al. 2025 (Whitehead; GSE295214) ran a genome-scale CRISPRi screen in H1 with 10x
5′ chemistry. The truth is the 2025 VCC H1 Flex data. On genes both priors cover:
- the other lab's programme (P_down over its 2,324 perturbations, h1-test targets excluded) reads 0.625 on gate-block
  genes, against 0.581 for CLEAN4 and 0.653 for the oracle, closing 62 % of the gap;
- per target, +0.057 [+0.024, +0.091], better on 37 of 49;
- its essential-gene perturbations alone carry the programme (0.627);
- its own rows for the 30 h1-test targets it covers reach gate signs 0.764 against 0.551 for the transfer model (26 of
  26 targets), and target-centred direction 0.260 against 0.069 (30 of 30).
- It replicates on a cancer line: with VIPerturb-seq K562 (Satija lab) as the truth, Replogle's K562
  screen beats the best cross-line bank 2.4× in direction on essential targets.

*Through the benchmark's own scorer.* Blending the other lab's rows into the transfer model's prediction for the 30
covered targets (35 % of the panel; production emitter; H1 surface) at weight 0.5 / 1 / 2 raises the six-member score by
**+0.053 / +0.071 / +0.079** in board units (mse −0.13 / −0.17 / −0.19 raw). With a per-target sign prior at weight 2 the
score is **+0.093** (3 seeds, seed sd 0.0002): mse −0.19, nmae −0.081, reach +0.076, pds +0.010. That is larger than any
single change in the record behind this paper.
- The final H1 price: **+0.093** at 35 % coverage. It uses weight 2, a per-target sign prior taken from the bank's own
  rows, and the cross-line consensus kept as the default block prior (3 seeds).
- The best weight falls with the bank's quality. With the bank's row signs degraded from 0.76 to 0.61 gate accuracy,
  the optimum moves from 2 to 0.5, and weight 2 then costs mse.
- The bank's pseudobulk norms do not carry each target's effect size: Spearman with the truth's energy +0.06, against
  +0.55 for the transfer model's own cross-donor key. Pan's direction reaches cosine 0.35 with the truth, ours 0.05. So
  the rows set direction and the transfer model sets size (454).

*The first scored test on a challenge context: null for the programme.*
- Context A of the validation board is Jurkat by expression match. Nadig 2025's Jurkat screen (Weissman lab; essential
  genes; 10x 3′) supplied A's block prior in place of the four-bank consensus. B and C, and every other part of the
  entry, were unchanged.
- The bulk members were bit-identical, as designed. Board fold-change sign fidelity moved +0.0002 raw against a
  preregistered +0.0015 … +0.0040, and the six-member score −0.0011 (mostly the noisy ordering member).
- The consensus already holds Jurkat at about a quarter weight, but on H1 a quarter-weight consensus keeps only 37 % of
  the same-line programme's gain.
- So the null concerns the bank: Nadig's Jurkat programme does not carry this Jurkat context the way Pan's H1
  programme carries VCC's H1. Lab, culture and screen design plausibly matter more for a suspension T-cell line.
- The per-target rows, which carry most of the H1 value, have no validation-board instrument: the Jurkat screen covers
  none of the non-essential panel.

*Why: among cancer lines the programme belongs to the lab* (Figure F-10). Per-gene P_down was computed for fifteen banks from
four labs: five Weissman-lab lines, two Xaira lines, six Jiang 2025 lines [JIANG25], one Satija K562 screen in Flex-style probe chemistry,
and a second HEK293-family screen.

Both programmes of a pair are computed over only the perturbations both banks screened, so a shared essential-gene set
cannot pose as a shared lab. Pairs sharing ≥ 1,000 perturbations:

| programme pair | pairs | mean r |
|---|---:|---:|
| same line, same lab | 1 | +0.81 |
| different line, same lab (Weissman, Xaira) | 10 | **+0.54** |
| same line, different lab (K562, Weissman vs Satija) | 2 | +0.21 |
| different line, different lab | 15 | +0.18 |

- Different lines from one lab agree more than the same line across labs. Lab and protocol add about +0.37 in r; the
  line adds a smaller ~+0.2, measured inside the one cross-lab K562 screen's own row, where its same-line partners beat
  its other-line partners on matched perturbation sets.
- An earlier comparison over each bank's whole screen (fifteen banks, four labs) put same-line cross-lab agreement near
  zero. That figure mixed essential, genome-wide and pathway screens, and is superseded.
- A chemistry-matched bank from another line does not substitute either. On the Flex H1 reference, the Flex-style K562
  programme signs gate-block genes at 0.539, below the four-bank consensus (0.581) and far below the same-line screen
  (0.625).
- H1's cross-lab transfer is therefore the exception that needs explaining, not the rule. An embryonic stem cell line
  has a strong, line-specific shared response to perturbation (exit from pluripotency) that plausibly dominates any lab
  signature.
- This is consistent with treating the shared component as a systematic, largely technical term, as Systema does. What
  our data add is where it lives: in the lab and protocol for these cancer lines, and in the line for H1.
- The per-target rows are a different object. On a common set of 1,129 targets and 6,083 genes, centred rows keep a line
  effect across labs.
  - Within the noisy cross-lab K562 screen's own pairs, its same-line partners read cosine 0.057 against 0.033 for its
    other-line partners (~1.7×).
  - Within one lab the same-line pair reads 0.216 against 0.129 (also ~1.7×).
  - With that screen as truth, the same-line bank beats a cross-line one 2.4× in direction on essential targets.
  - So the target-specific response carries line information across labs, while the shared programme does not.

*Estimating a programme from one screen: its control is part of it* (Figure F-11). Every target in a screen is measured against the
same control cells. So the control sample's deviation from its population enters every response as a common per-gene
offset, which is exactly what a programme is. Splitting the targets in half cannot see this, because both halves share
the control. Splitting the cells, with disjoint halves of the controls, can (462).
- **Broad-16** (Cas9 knockout, one lab, 12 working cancer lines, ~500 control cells per line against 43–129 per target,
  82 matched targets): the split-half reliability of P_down is 0.80 with a shared control and **0.12** with disjoint
  controls. Honestly measured, lines within the lab share about a third of their programme: cross-line 0.043 against
  within-line 0.121.
- **Arc's H1 harness bank** (400 cells per target, 18,399 control rows): 0.79 against **0.39** on expressed genes, so
  about a third of its variance is one control sample's noise. The same programme from all of Arc's public H1 cells
  (~1,071 per target, 38,176 controls) reads 0.69 disjoint.
  - On H1's own reference, with the test targets excluded, the clean programme's signs match the recipient oracle: 0.5545
    against 0.5544 on all block genes, and 0.640 against 0.625 on significant ones. CLEAN4 reads 0.5244 and 0.573.
  - Through the production emitter on H1 (the block prior swapped, 3 seeds), it doubles the other lab's gains: fold-change
    sign fidelity +0.0198 raw and fold-change error −0.0148, against Pan's +0.0096 and −0.0086.
  - It pays the same reach cost (−0.039). The same four small-gate targets carry it for both programmes, targets whose
    few true DE genes move against the line's common programme. A per-gene prior cannot know such exceptions.
  - **A design-strength gate removes the point-estimate reach cost; its own benefit is not established (467; the
    target-bootstrap interval spans 0).** A target's strength is the number of genes whose designed effect
    exceeds 0.2 inside the emission gate (Spearman 0.48 with its true DE count). The programme goes to the strongest two
    thirds of targets and the cross-line consensus to the rest.
    - Fidelity +0.0161 raw, fold-change error −0.0102, reach +0.0015: net +0.0110 against +0.0067 ungated.
    - Over targets, the gated programme's gain is robust: 95 % [+0.0067, +0.0158] by a target bootstrap. The gate's own
      benefit is not established: +0.0043, [−0.0034, +0.0132]. It rests on the four exception targets, and without them
      it reverses. The seed spread (0.0002) measures only emission noise.
    - All four exception targets fall in the weak third.
    - For the weak third, reach ranks the consensus (+0.0015) above the design's own signs (−0.0099) and the line's
      programme (−0.0388). A consensus sign is right more often on the few genes a weak response moves.
    - The strength measure and the share were chosen on H1, so this is in-sample. A same-seed board pair tests it.
    - It matters most on an essential-free panel, which is what the 2026 benchmark is. On h1-test's 67 non-essential
      targets, the ungated programme nets +0.0015: fidelity +0.0138 is almost cancelled by reach −0.0497. Gated, it
      keeps +0.0078. The programme's fidelity gain is three times larger on essential targets (+0.042 per target vs
      +0.014).
    - The four exception targets' true DE genes are nearly all down (AASS 15/16, ZNF664 7/7, CNN3 and COL6A1 1/1). A
      weak CRISPRi response leans down in every public line we checked. Proxy DE sets on six banks give down fractions
      of 0.58–0.67 per weak perturbation (K562, RPE1, Jurkat, HCT116, HEK293T, H1), and the consensus prior is itself
      mostly down.
- **It does not inflate sign agreement on significant genes.** The leave-one-target-out oracle above reads 0.6184 with a
  shared control half and 0.6183 with disjoint halves, so the gap to it is recipient signal.
- So judge a programme source with control halves, and build it from every cell and control a screen has.
- **Where the lab signature lives.** In CRISPRi with cells (Replogle's K562 and RPE1 essential screens, one lab and
  library, 2,055 matched targets), the honest cross-line agreement is 0.45: 0.53 of the within-line reliability, against
  0.35 for the Broad knockouts.
  - On the 1,309 essential targets that five banks from three labs share, other labs' lines agree almost as well as
    the same lab's (mean r 0.38 vs 0.43). Knocking down an essential gene has a strong, common biological response.
  - The lab component reported above (0.54 vs 0.18) comes from broad target sets, where most responses are weak.
  - **With single cells and disjoint controls on genome-wide screens from two labs** (Xaira HCT116 and HEK293T,
    Weissman K562; 1,500 non-essential and 500 essential matched targets, ≥ 50 cells each):
    - on non-essential targets two lines from one lab agree at 0.17–0.18, against 0.05–0.09 across labs;
    - on essential targets other labs' lines agree at least as well as the same lab's (0.41–0.52 vs 0.35–0.43).
    - So a programme is a lab signature where responses are weak and shared biology where they are strong.
  - That is the regime of a non-essential benchmark panel, and it is where a same-lab programme could help.

*The negatives that bound it.*
- A same-tissue line from another lab (DLD-1 for HCT116, both colorectal and MSI-high) does not beat cross-line banks.
  The best predictor of HCT116 is HEK293T from the same lab and protocol.
- A same-line L1000 shRNA signature set (CMap HepG2 for Nadig's HepG2 Perturb-seq) is worse than other lines' L1000
  signatures (−0.018 [−0.026, −0.011] per target).
- The benefit needs the same line and a Perturb-seq-grade measurement.

*Target-specific public sources do not supply the missing signs (516; C-79).* If the recipient's programme is out of reach,
can other screens' rows for the same target recover the uncalled genes' signs? No construction passed the bar (+0.02 in

- The best construction is a Stouffer consensus of six same-target public rows (K562, two Orion lines, three CD4 conditions),

  targets.
- On three cancer lines held out cross-study (no source from the held line or its study), it reads +0.0033 [+0.0004,
  +0.0062] (HCT116), −0.0002 [−0.0027, +0.0023] (HEK293T) and +0.0010 [−0.0030, +0.0053] (K562) against the production
  analogue.
- The null is not power: re-thinning H1's truth to 100–250 cells, or to the folds' depth, leaves the H1 gain at +0.015 …
  +0.018.
- Mechanism: about +0.017 of the H1 gain is the carriers' whitening. Unwhitened donor signs are better on H1's weak,
  uncalled genes, and the same unwhitened-carrier family inverted nmae on the board (+0.0089 raw, 417c). The remaining
  +0.007 … +0.014 is the consensus, which the cancer folds reproduce at 0.000 ± 0.005.
- Ceilings: an oracle choice of the best single public source per target reaches +0.030 pooled on H1. Supervised stacking
  over seven sources adds ≤ +0.002, and weights learned on the cancer folds invert H1's.
- So "sign sources are at the public-data limit" now covers target-specific combinations too, at these constructions.

*Nor do recipient-side or technical features (517; C-80).*

### 3(k) What a scorer built on two functionals of the same counts lets a submission do

**F19 — The benchmark's six members read two different functionals of the submitted counts, and a submission can set
them almost independently. Provenance: the functionals are the scorer's documented definitions (KNOWN). That they can be
decoupled by the per-cell library structure of a submission, and the size of the decoupling on the scored board, are
candidate NEW, pending audit. Sources: experiments 389, 390, 410, 417, 433, 446; `docs/BOARD-LEDGER.md`.**

*The two functionals.*
- The retrieval and profile-error members (pds, mse) read each perturbation's log-pseudobulk: its cells' counts summed,
  then normalised.
- The four differential-expression members read per-cell quantities: arithmetic means of per-cell counts-per-million,
  and Wilcoxon ranks of the 400 cells against the real controls.
- A sum is dominated by deep cells. A per-cell mean and a rank give every cell a weight of 1/400.

*The construction.* A few "ballast" cells per perturbation (median 4 to 6) are written at about 995,000 counts each.
That is inside the format's cap of 1,000,000 per cell, but 19× above the deepest real control (52,420). They carry
roughly 40 % of the summed counts, so they set the pseudobulk to the bulk prediction. The other ~395 cells carry the
differential-expression design at realistic depth. A pair construction keeps the profile-error member's jackknife
correction from penalising the ballast.

- The exact-rank DE change left all five other members bit-identical to 16 figures, fid, reach and jac included. That is the
  strongest form of C-62.
- The repair of zeroed genes is not a pure DE change: it edits the totals of the few genes it repairs, and the bulk moved by
  0.00007 raw.

*What the profile-error member reads once the bulk is free (518; C-84, C-85, C-93).*
- Each context is re-levelled to its cliff, the level (set from control cells) at which the member's jackknife credit
  exactly offsets the prediction's spread energy. There the member's raw value is 1.032 − 2X/ΣD. X is the energy-weighted
  agreement of predicted and true responses, and ΣD the truth's summed energy. The constant carries the mean row and the
  observed controls' noise (2.2–2.6 % of ΣD).
- The identity rebuilds H1's raw member exactly from its measured terms. On the board, solving it for X from 446bj2's raw
  value gives 2X/ΣD = 0.406. Spread energy is free and only direction pays: +1 % relative X is −0.0041 raw, +0.0007
  overall.
- At the same constants, the leaders' raw values imply +13.3 % … +13.8 % (two teams) and +33.6 % (the leader) more aggregate
  cosine than ours. That assumes their mean-row and control terms equal ours.
- The magnitude pot: at our directions, a per-target magnitude oracle raises X by +23.2 / +25.1 / +27.1 % on three held-out
  cancer lines (+52 % on an iPSC line, +26.5 % on H1). That is −0.10 … −0.13 raw, +0.018 … +0.023 overall, and pds-free by
  construction. Truly strong targets are under-scaled (median ideal-to-current scale 1.31 in H1's top decile by true energy).
- One transferred step came from that pot. A leave-one-donor-out (LODO) key weights a target by how strongly each donor's row agrees
  with the prediction built without that donor. It moved mse −0.014473 raw on the board (446bj2), past its preregistered
  −0.004 … −0.012, while pds moved +0.0002 against +0.001 … +0.004.
- After a raised cap on that key and a response-strength key (both priced on local folds only; the cap's board read is
  pending), about 20–23 % remains. Half of it sits in 11–19 targets per held-out line that no donor-side, CD4, VIPerturb, KOLF2, expression or
  cross-fold feature predicts out of fold.
- The direction pot is far larger: on H1 the per-target direction oracle at our energies takes the aggregate cosine from 0.113
  to 0.818. It is the information limit of §3(h).

*How much a realistic-library guard would remove.*
- The ballast must carry a mass comparable to the carriers': ~395 cells at a ~20,000-count median, about 7.9M counts.
- At the format's cap (1,000,000 per cell), 4–6 cells suffice, about 1.5 % of each perturbation's per-cell weight.
- Capped at the controls' largest library (52,420 counts), the same mass would need ~150 cells, or 38 % of the per-cell
  means and ranks.
- So a cap at the reference's observed range removes the decoupling almost entirely.

*Why it matters for benchmark design.* A scorer that evaluates members on different functionals of the same counts
implicitly assumes submitted cells resemble real cells. When they need not, each member can be optimised on its own.
Two cheap guards would close it: bound each predicted cell's library to the reference's observed range, or compute
every member from the same per-cell-normalised representation. We report this as a property of the evaluation, used
within the published format contract and disclosed here, not as a biological result.

### 3(l) What the rank and set members reward

**F20 — The rank member (reach) is limited by signs on weak responses, not by the order of the calls. The call set's size is
a bet on the reference's distribution of response sizes, and per-target information cannot hedge it across lines.
Provenance: candidate NEW (a property of the scorer at this pipeline's design), pending audit. Confirmation: analytic on H1
with exact known-answer rows (513, 521); a board dose pair on the global call-set size is preregistered (523) and unread.**

*Where reach lives (513; C-86).* Reach scores how deep a prediction's ranking of the reference's significant genes stays at
≥ 90 % sign purity. On H1, from the production emitter's saved statistics (the four DE members reproduced to 2 × 10⁻¹⁶):
- targets with fewer than 20 significant genes (32 of 82) score 0.643, with a walk sign accuracy of 0.81;
- 20–100 significant genes (9 targets) score 0.078 (walk accuracy 0.51); 100–1,000 (19) score 0.027; ≥ 1,000 (22) score
  0.014. An estimate from the board's members puts the challenge contexts' median target at 31–45 significant genes (192;
  disputed below), in the band where this pipeline gets 0.08.
- Sign accuracy decays down the pipeline's own order, from 0.80 in its first ten ranks to 0.55 beyond rank 250 (targets with
  ≥ 100 significant genes). The order carries confidence but never reaches the 0.9 the walk needs. Ordering by the truth's
  own |fold change| with our signs is worse (0.234).
- Demoting the high-expression block, whose signs (0.58 accurate in the 20–100 band) crowd the head, loses at every block
  size and scheme: Δreach −0.0007 … −0.038. Behind the ~2 per-target genes per target that are 0.95 accurate, the rest are at
  chance.
- No identifiable subset of useful size is pure. Genes on which ≥ 3 donors agree with |consensus| ≥ 1 log2 are 0.972
  accurate but number 72 over 82 targets, almost all in pools of ≥ 100.

So reach is sign-limited, not order-limited. Rivals' reach moved by +0.03 raw with their means pinned (fixed-mean rank
edits); such an edit needs a head several genes deep at ≥ 0.9 purity, which the public sources supply only for the strongest
targets.

*Sizing the call set per target (521; C-87).* A response-strength key (a noise-normalised count of strongly responding genes
in the donors' own rows) predicts H1's number of significant genes at r +0.60 (K562 alone) and +0.55 (four sources). The
earlier design predictor reached +0.24, and the key's accuracy does not depend on the truth's power.
- On held-out cancer lines it predicts only r +0.43 (K562), +0.25 … +0.27 (HCT116) and +0.19 … +0.25 (HEK293T); R² 0.02–0.18.
- The payoff is asymmetric. Shrinking a negligible target's call set to 10 genes gains +0.0004 overall on average; shrinking
  a non-negligible one loses −0.0034 (8.5 : 1). A shrink therefore needs 89–97 % of its targets to be negligible.
- The cancer lines put 7–46 % of their weakest-ranked 5–10 % of targets in the truth's top half, and every small-call-set
  policy prices −0.0005 … −0.016 on every fold.
- Per-target sizing stays closed, now by its payoff rather than by its input.

*The global size is a regime bet (521, 523; C-88).* fid rewards covering the reference's significant genes and jac penalises
over-calling, and their balance moves with the distribution of response sizes.
- On H1 a global call set of 1,600 genes instead of 2,600 is −0.0155 [−0.0251, −0.0072] overall at the reference's own
  power, and +0.0048 [+0.0009, +0.0100] with the reference thinned to 35 % of its significant genes.
- The board's regime is disputed: a fit to fid's coverage term says ~0.5, the jac level ~0.33. So the answer is a board
  measurement: a preregistered dose pair (1,600 and 2,000 genes called per target, DE-only, pds and mse pinned) is
  scheduled from 10-06 and unread.
- By-products. The board's weak end is thinner than proportional thinning assumes (nmae cohorts put 13–30 % of targets below
  10 gate genes, against H1's 32–42 %), so small-pool levers are over-priced on thinned H1. And "an unexpressed target has a
  null response" is refuted: panel targets below 5 CPM in a line's controls are null 26 % of the time on the K562 fold (11 of
  42), about as often as expressed ones (23 %).

*Why it matters for benchmark design.* Two of the four DE members turn on quantities a model cannot learn from public data.
Reach turns on the few significant genes of weak responses, where it needs ≥ 90 % sign purity. The fid/jac balance turns on
the reference's distribution of response sizes. A benchmark that wants its DE channel to reward biology rather than
calibration could report reach by response-size band and fix the call-set size relative to the reference's own counts.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 What is defensible as new

Four items, each with its caveats in the same sentence (287 §2, Tier 1).

1. **Rank retrieval needs the noise-dominated directions of its own whitening operator (F3b).** Damping the directions where an independent noise estimate exceeds the fitted eigenvalue — the standard shrinkage prescription — loses retrieval on 3 of 3 folds at every damping strength; caveats: one operator family, one metric, three folds, a mechanism that is inferred rather than directly measured, and an eigenvalue-bias half of the story that is textbook and cited as such.
2. **Cross-line conservation does not predict within-individual state dependence, though it does track the across-donor ceiling (F11c).** Preregistered, with the preregistered index retracted honestly; caveats: 74 targets, ~66–107 cells per side, one cell type, and a deconfounded interval that covers zero — so "no relation detected", not "no relation".
3. **The biological donor is a marginal replicate unit relative to cells, and replicates beat cells at a matched budget in CD4 T cells (F10b).** Caveats: four people, one run-clean condition, a guide-split comparator, and a live tension with a 26-donor iPSC design that finds donor effects heritable.
4. **A family-resolved, preregistered, magnitude- and reliability-controlled cross-line conservation axis (F9),** strengthened by an independent reproduction inside the CD4 source's own table — including that the corroborating interval clears zero in only one of three conditions. New as a *quantification*; the core-machinery-versus-cell-identity ordering is what the co-essentiality and lineage-dependency literature expects.

The audit's extension (297; `experiments/297-audit-extension/AUDIT-EXTENSION.md`) graded the post-audit work — **NEW 3, EXTENDED 7, KNOWN 5, CONTRADICTED 0**. **NEW:** 284's reproducible per-gene remainder (the load-bearing one — it converts "our features cannot predict the remainder" into "the remainder is a reproducible gene property"); F14's inversion that a different study in the same line agrees better than a second construct in the same study; F13's identity-versus-geometry ablation. **EXTENDED:** 290's source-disjoint construction at its ceiling; 293's selection effect on two-construct targets and its construct-level (rather than cell-split) ceiling; 286's estimator control on F3b; C5's preregistered off-map validation (PerturbMap's prospective reliability weighting is the prior art; a per-gene score validated on never-opened studies is not); the cells-and-depth equivalence for a pseudobulk statistic; batch free. **KNOWN:** F13's sign (Wang et al. 2026), the Spearman–Brown reliability law (Wang et al. 2026), the construct as the dominant term (the CD4 source's own column), the line / state / donor ladder, and knockdown failure as the mechanism. Of the five post-audit results the two biggest headlines are known; the genuinely new content is narrower than the findings note implied, and this paper says so.

The strongest confirmation the programme produced is C5: a map built from 28 public banks predicts, at **ρ +0.50**, how well a gene's response actually transfers into eight datasets that no part of the programme had ever opened, across a perturbation modality and a cell type the map never saw, with the test frozen before the first byte of expression data was read.

And one framing result that is not any single finding: **the three-way separation of repeatability, replicate agreement and transfer** (§1.2), at +0.243, +0.054 and +0.020 (three measurements on different cells).

**The one result that postdated both audits has since been audited, and nothing in it is new: F16 (§3(g)).** 320 graded it **KNOWN 4, EXTENDED 1, NEW 0.** The phenomenon ([ROHATGI24]; [HARTMAN26]), the seed-plus-promoter rule and its measured predictive precision, the partner-guide specificity control ([JACKSON03]; [HARTMAN26]) and the aggregate-over-per-item statistical logic are all published. What is ours is the design — construct as the unit, with a joint null over candidate sets matched on a measured library-wide off-target propensity — and a re-measurement of the phenomenon on the challenge's own library, whose specificity its producers did not assess. It therefore belongs in §4.2 with the other re-measurements and not in this section; an earlier revision of this paragraph reserved judgement on its novelty, and the reservation is now spent. The finding's honesty condition sits inside the result rather than beside it: the first analysis met both of its pass conditions and aborted itself on two of its own known-answer rows, and repairing exactly those two moved the estimate down 9.5 %.

**Three later candidates, not yet audited (2026-09-26), stated with their caveats.**
- **The shared response programme is mostly a lab and protocol signature among cancer lines, while target-specific
  rows keep a line signal across labs (F18, C-64/C-65).**
  - Caveats: eight banks from three labs. The one cross-lab same-line pair is a noisy screen (median 48 cells per target).
    The embryonic line H1 is an exception. No interval is attached.
  - The closest prior work names the component (Systema); what is candidate-new is where it lives.
  - **[[PENDING 09-30]]** The direct test of the laboratory component on the challenge's own lines is the producer's H1
    programme as every context's block prior (458d) and its gate (458g), §3(j).
- **The benchmark's members read two functionals of the same counts, and a submission can set them apart (F19, C-61/
  C-62).**
  - Caveats: a property of this scorer and format contract, measured on one team's entries.
  - It is disclosed here as an evaluation-design finding with two cheap guards, not as a biological result.
- **A per-gene programme estimated from one screen partly measures that screen's control sample, and target
  split-halves hide it (C-66–C-69).**
  - Caveats: three designs (Broad-16 KO, Arc's H1 Flex, Replogle's K562/RPE1 CRISPRi), one seeded split each, no
    intervals. That essential-target programmes are mostly biology rests on five banks from three labs.
  - Why it matters: any "universal response" or programme prior estimated from a single screen needs control halves
    to be judged.

**Later candidates, not yet audited (2026-09-30), stated with their caveats.**
- **The uncalled-gene hook's bar (C-76, C-77).** A same-lab programme can pay on uncalled genes where it cannot pay as a
  block prior, because there it has to beat the design's own signs (0.53 on H1) rather than a cross-line consensus.
  - Caveats: H1 only, the programme's own line, so an upper reference. The estimator was chosen on the same instruments.
    The attenuation is an emulation (random sign flips, not a real change of line). The cross-line analogue rests on two
    laboratories. The board read is pending (527).
- **Reach is sign-limited, and per-target call-set sizing does not transfer across lines (F20, C-86, C-87).**
  - Caveats: an analytic walk over one pipeline's order on H1, with exact known answers; the cancer folds' truths are
    150-cell pseudobulks. The global size's direction on the board is unread (523).
- **At its cliff, the profile-error member reads the aggregate cosine (C-84).**
  - Caveats: an identity under this pipeline's re-levelling and ballast construction. The rivals' implied cosines assume
    their mean-row and control terms equal ours.

### 4.2 What is re-measurement, credited to its source

Five items are publishable only as re-measurement (287 §2, Tier 2, plus F16 from 320), and the framing matters as much as the numbers.

- **F16's seed off-target association (§3(g))** re-measures, on the challenge's own 2025 library, a phenomenon [ROHATGI24] demonstrated two years earlier in that library's own parent hCRISPRi-v2 collection — with screens, RNA-seq **and** dCas9 ChIP-seq occupancy — and [HARTMAN26] systematised across three genome-wide Perturb-seq datasets. The seed-plus-promoter rule, its window, its 12-nt threshold, its published low precision and the partner-guide specificity control ([JACKSON03]) are all theirs. Ours is the design — a recombination of [SEEDMATCHR24]'s aggregate seed-shift test with [MATCHRANGES23]'s covariate-matched null, new only in using a *measured* 209,070-spacer propensity as the matching covariate — and the sample: this library's specificity had not been assessed by its producers.

- **F1's reliability law** reproduces, on the challenge's own panel with a residual-cosine estimator, the published result that most perturbation responses in genome-scale screens are not reproducible ([ORION], [REPLOGLE22], [WANG26], [PAN26]) — and must distinguish itself from Orion's NTC-profile Spearman of 0.99.
- **F6's DE sparsity** is Replogle's own published 30.5 %: with the scorer's Wilcoxon–Benjamini–Hochberg recipe and matched controls, the median K562 panel target has **5 significant genes**, 85 of 272 (31 %) clearing a 10-gene bar. It carries [NADIG25]'s caveat that DEG counts *understate* the true effect.
- **F5a's decomposition** is new in form; its two named programmes are the source paper's own.
- **F1c and F11b's construct effect** is published by the CD4 source, with the mechanism (*"differential knockdown efficiency between guides"*); ours is the crossed 2 × 2 at matched depth.

### 4.3 An amendment this synthesis makes to its own record

F14 called the guide construct "the dominant irreducible term of a perturbation response". Measured in the challenge's own library (293), the term is **almost entirely knockdown failure** (+0.687 where both constructs reach |lfc| 1.0, −0.003 where one does not). Applied to the five public libraries (296), the stratification holds in Nadig Jurkat, holds at a library-relative cut in K562 with magnitude riding along, is not separated in HepG2, is absent in Broad-16 (Cas9), and is a powered null in primary CD4 T cells. The record therefore reads: **guide efficacy is the dominant term in immortalised-line CRISPRi libraries and the challenge's own; in primary T cells the construct term is there whether or not the guides work; and once a cross-gene null is subtracted the public CRISPRi construct terms are all small (≈ 0.03–0.08), with only the Cas9 library's surviving.** F14's construct ladder is a lower bound throughout (two-construct targets are every library's weakest) and its cross-library ordering partly ranked shared biology.

### 4.4 What is explicitly not claimed

- **F7 (board calibration constants)** is engineering calibration of an unpublished scorer from single board points. It is not a scientific claim and does not appear outside §3(h).
- **F16 does not establish off-target binding, nothing biological in it is new, and C8 plus C9 are not two experiments.** What is measured is an aggregate expression association, on one experiment's cells, with the construct as the unit at n = 34 and no biological replicate of any construct; the partner-construct control is a standard one borrowed from [JACKSON03] and [HARTMAN26], attaches the association to the protospacer sequence, and cannot separate seed-mediated binding from a construct artefact that travels with spacer identity. The headline p = 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ is a **gene-sampling** p, not a construct-level one; the construct-level readings are the bootstrap interval and the paired partner Wilcoxon (§3(g)). The failing-versus-working contrast the association was originally sought to explain remains unsupported (T7).
- **C1 is a robustness check, not a confirmation.** Half B was inside the parent evaluation.
- **F11a's "state removes about half" ratio** is not established: the absolute difference is +0.0080 [−0.0026, +0.0186].
- **F4a's knee is not a discovery**; it is largely a property of the decoy distribution.
- **"A spectral feature set rather than a pathway-level one"** is withdrawn outside the map: on new surfaces annotation and geometry are two partly independent views (ρ(geometry, outcome | annotation) +0.16 / +0.11) and annotation is as good or better.
- **The depth ceiling** ("cells are not the binding constraint at any genome-scale budget") is retracted and contradicted; F14 replaces it with a measured law.
- **Claim C6** (overexpression reverses knockdown in hESC) is VOID, not decided: the reserve was spent under a rule its own known-answer row voided; what is reported is a bounded null.
- **No claim that public data cannot supply fold-change signs in general.** 516 closes the constructions it tested:
  target-specific public rows; their Stouffer, majority and stacked combinations over up to seven sources; and denoised and
  related-target-borrowed rows. It tested them on H1 and on three cross-study cancer folds, whose truths are 150-cell
  pseudobulks (two carry a technical component). 517 closes three target-independent sources at their constructions: the
  identity axis, library-size composition and dropout.
- **No claim about any competitor's method** beyond what its opened code does (525). The six-team `cellpred` cluster is
  private and its fold-change edge unexplained. §3(i)'s mapping of the leaders' nmae to sign accuracy is an upper bound on
  what they need, not a measurement of their signs (C-59).
- **Per-target call-set sizing is not claimed as a lever**, and its H1 predictability (r 0.55–0.60) is not claimed as a
  property of cancer lines (r 0.19–0.43; C-87). The global size's sign on the board is unknown until 523 reads.
- **H1 prices of DE sign sources are not board claims.** 517's hook (nmae −0.0183), 517's block prior (+0.0169
  overall-equivalent, one seed, estimator chosen after seeing reach) and 516's +0.016 are H1 numbers. Sign priors have
  transferred 0.07–0.67× of their H1 fid to the board, and their reach and nmae effects are not priceable on H1 (T9).
- **The pipeline refit's equality (C-82) is a statement about A/B/C relabelled**, not about a new panel. The test panel's
  composition (CD4 coverage, the bulk key's tails; 438, 522) is what the day-of rules price, on local instruments only.

### 4.5 Contradictions and tensions

- **T1 — F3b against shrinkage-regularised whitening.** A tension, not a contradiction: the prior work optimises semantic similarity or classification on embeddings; ours is a 300-way scale-invariant *rank retrieval* in which the discriminating signal sits, by construction, in the low-variance directions. The one like-for-like comparison supports both: the soft floor c = 1 is null on the rank metric and **+4.4 %** on aggregate cosine — the standard prescription helps the similarity objective and not the retrieval one, as that literature's own task-dependence caveat predicts.
- **T2 — F10b's marginal donor term against heritable donor modulation at 26 donors [IPSC34].** Different cell type, different n, different statistic. Not resolvable on the data we have; we name it.
- **T3 — F8's conservation score and response magnitude.** In the source's own table conservation tracks the MASH DE count at ρ +0.54. Measured directly (288), about 40 % of F8's association is magnitude and the rest survives partialling; in the weakest magnitude tertile the association is **at chance**. Any use of a conservation score must carry the magnitude stratification.
- **T4 — a procedural failure.** C2a's pass rule was frozen on an unconditioned threshold although the source paper had already published the UMI–power law, and the reserved quarter was the shallow quarter. The lesson binds every future preregistration on multi-line data (rule 9: stratify or covariate-adjust on UMIs per perturbation, and read the source's own reliability statistics before freezing).
- **T6 — F14's cells-and-depth equivalence against the sequencing-budget literature.** "One axis at equal total UMIs" runs against the headline that one should maximise cells at ≈ one read per cell per gene [DEPTH-TSE] and against a depth-first regime below ≈ 15 k reads per cell [SVENSSON-DEPTH]. Different objective (pseudobulk response reproducibility versus per-cell estimation and clustering); stated in the same sentence wherever the equivalence is quoted.
- **T7 — what a failing construct's reproducible response is made of: partly answered at the aggregate level, still open per construct.**
  The state after Round 6 has three parts, and the first two are negatives that stand.
  **(i) 301's failing-versus-working negative stands.** Its seed scan found no enrichment of
  candidate promoter off-targets among failing constructs — 4 of 13 failing against 4 of 21
  working, 4 of 31 non-targeting, Fisher p = 0.68 — and 307's known-answer row reproduced that
  selection exactly, so the count-level negative is not an artefact of the scan. The aggregate
  re-reading agrees: neither C8 nor C9 finds a failing-versus-working contrast (−0.077
  [−0.869, +0.699] and +0.1949 [−0.5584, +0.9434], opposite signs, both intervals several times
  the difference).
  **(ii) 305 produced no accepted discovery.** Its eleven sequence-frozen construct–gene pairs
  gave two nominal signals, `GRB10_P2 → SNX8` and `DPF2_P1 → CEP120`, at full-family Holm
  p = 0.10476 — zero of eleven pass — and the design could not have attained its own family-wise
  error rate in the first place: with the 8 pairs that turned out testable and an empirical rank-p
  floor of 0.0063, the smallest attainable Holm-adjusted p was **0.0506 > 0.05**. Its post-hoc
  base-rate rescue is not an accepted confirmatory test. That verdict is unchanged.
  **(iii) F16 (§3(g)) supplies what 305 could not**: a measured aggregate association with the
  **construct** as the estimand's unit and a null whose attainable floor is 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ rather than
  0.0696, passing at S = +0.713 [+0.324, +1.074] after a calibration repair that moved the estimate
  down 9.5 % — a re-measurement of a published phenomenon (320: NEW 0), not a discovery, and with
  a headline p that resamples genes rather than constructs.
  **301's negative and F16 are not in conflict, and the paper should not be read as if they were:
  they answer different questions — 301 asks whether candidates are enriched among *failing*
  constructs (they are not), F16 asks whether candidates are repressed *in aggregate across all*
  constructs (they are).** What remains open is everything causal: no occupancy measurement, no
  biological replicate of any construct, no separation of seed-mediated binding from a
  spacer-correlated construct artefact, and no 2026 off-target rate. The earlier recommendation of
  a sequence-mutant control as *the decisive next experiment* is withdrawn: [ROHATGI24] already
  demonstrates the mechanism at the level of dCas9 binding, with occupancy data, in this library's
  own parent collection, so a new experiment here would buy local attribution on 34 constructs
  rather than a mechanism the literature lacks (§3(g)). No further analysis of these cells settles
  it either.
- **T8 — F14's study ratio 0.79 against Replogle 2022's same-line cross-experiment r 0.50.** Settled (298): both statistics on the same pair give the same inversion (raw cosine +0.168 study versus +0.011 construct on 112 shared perturbations), and the distance from our pooled +0.215 to Replogle's ≈ 0.50 is the cohort — the strongest-responding third reads +0.411.
- **T5 — F2 against a published positive.** "Selection cannot precede estimation" reads as contradicting [WANG26], who find that selecting *training* perturbations by reliability *helps* (matching full data with 55 % of the rows). It does not. F2 rejects **per-target selection of a donor row by its proximity to the truth**; it says nothing against reliability-based filtering of training data. Naming the operation is the whole fix.
- **T9 — local prices against the board, 2026-09-27 … 09-29.** The six reads since the H1-to-board calibration table
  (`docs/H1-BOARD-CALIBRATION.md`, 2026-09-26) was compiled; raw members, arm minus base, predictions as preregistered:

  the board (417c: −0.0068 predicted on H1, +0.0089 raw on the board). This is not a contradiction inside 516: its
  cross-study cancer folds read the same ≈ 0 that the board implies.

### 4.6 Limitations

One map, one statistic (a residual cosine on a 2,739-gene common axis), and shared processing pipelines across the halves of every split-half test we ran — a gene-level technical covariate correlated with gene identity across studies is not excluded. The n's are small where they matter most: 4 donors, 5–10 two-construct targets on the Flex chemistry (though 748 on K562), 12 working cell lines on Broad-16, 19–21 lines in the map, 3 PLOLO folds, 8 files as the blocks behind C5, and **34 constructs — one experiment's cells, one shared control pool, no biological replicate — behind F16, where the interval is set by the number of constructs and no amount of further sequencing would narrow it**. Reliability corrections use Spearman–Brown on split halves and inherit its assumptions. Broad-16's guide term is measured on a design whose cell split mixes constructs, and its Cas9 modality is a hypothesis rather than a controlled contrast. The CD4 bank's cell-sampling reference is a count split corrected by a transferred additive offset (+0.044). And, most importantly for the motivating setting: **the reliability, conservation and construct results are not measured on the challenge's held contexts A/B/C**. The programme tests (§3(j)), the two-functional decomposition (§3(k)), the pipeline refit (§3(h)) and the second fold-change delivery (§3(i)) are, as scored board entries on those three contexts. §3(l) and the H1 prices in §3(i)–(j) are measured on H1 and public folds only. Every other statement about A/B/C is an extrapolation from other cell types, and this programme's own record contains two claims that had to be retracted for exactly that kind of extrapolation.

## 5. Methods

**5.1 The response object and the scored statistic.** For target *t*, the prediction *p_t* is a bulk log1p(50,000 · P / ΣP) profile minus the same profile of the real control cells, with the panel's target-gene columns removed; on PLOLO it is rendered through the production `profile_from_effect` into the challenge control frames and read back as the scorer does. The rank statistic is `pds_t` = [#{q ≠ t : cos(p_t, y_q) < cos(p_t, y_t)} + ½ #{ties}] / (n − 1) over the held truths y, averaged over frames on PLOLO; a fold's score is the mean over its n targets, so one rank step is 1/(n − 1) per frame. This implementation reproduces `cell-eval2`'s per-target `pds_cosine` on a cloud-scored artifact to **5.6 × 10⁻¹⁷**, the earlier reliability rows at 0.0 × 10⁰ and a union-free composition at 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁷. "Own cosine" is cos(p_t, y_t) on the scored columns; `cos_agg` is an energy-weighted aggregate cosine.

**5.2 Residual space and split units.** Each side's profile minus the mean over that side's targets (the bank-wide mean on PLOLO's instrument). Splits: batch or gemgroup parity (even vs odd) with controls split by the same rule; random cell halves (one draw per cell, seed 20260905); guide constructs P1 vs P2 where two exist; Flex lanes from the 48-value barcode suffix. Spearman–Brown 2r/(1 + r) converts a half-depth reliability to full depth. On Broad-16, the cells of each (line, knockout) are split at random and each half scored against the same half of ten odorant-receptor control knockouts; noise is the energy the other half does not reproduce, halved to convert half depth to full depth (checked at 0.4996).

**5.3 The conservation statistic (DEFINITION-276, frozen before any cosine).** A *source* is one (dataset, cell line[, cell state]) with a compact donor table on disk; aggregates over lines, mouse tables, CRISPRa tables and sources carrying fewer than 5 panel targets are excluded. The common gene axis is the intersection over included sources of measured symbols, intersected with the challenge's 18,533 genes; the panel's own target-gene columns are **not** removed (the target gene's own knockdown is part of the response direction; the removed variant is a declared secondary). For target *t*, `C_raw(t)` is the mean over all pairs of sources from **different** cell lines that both carry *t* of cos(R_s[t], R_s′[t]); same-line pairs are excluded from the primary because they measure reproducibility rather than conservation, and reported separately as `C_within`. `C_rel(t)` divides each pair's cosine by √(r_s r_s′) with r the Spearman–Brown full-depth reliability, dropping pairs where either r ≤ 0.02. `C_lolo` recomputes `C_raw` with the recipient line's own sources removed. The null is target-shuffled pairings that preserve the pair structure and the gene sets exactly, 200 draws.

**5.4 The reliability ratio ρ (285 §6).** The observed agreement of any unit at n cells per side is ρ_unit · a(n), where a(n) is the cell-sampling attenuation. Because a same-construct cell split has ρ = 1 by construction, the cell-split row **is** a(n), and ρ_unit = cos_unit(n) / cos_cells(n) at the same n. ρ is therefore what more measurement can never change, and the n at which more cells stop helping solves a(n) = ρ through Spearman–Brown from the measured base rung. For the CD4 bank, whose object has no accessible cells, the cell-sampling reference is a molecular count split, which is Poisson-only and optimistic; on the two sources that have both, the count-minus-cell offset at matched total UMIs is nearly constant (+0.0490 / +0.0445 / +0.0459 / +0.0443 on h1-test; +0.0398 / +0.0415 / +0.0416 on K562; mean **+0.0438**), and the CD4 ρ's subtract it.

**5.5 Intervals, permutations and multiplicity.** Paired target bootstrap, 20,000 draws, stratified by fold, percentile [2.5, 97.5], with P(Δ ≤ 0) reported; 268 and 283 use 4,000 draws over targets carrying their folds; 276 and 290/291 use 2,000, with component (target-cluster) block bootstraps where the unit is genes and family components induce dependence. Permutations: drop-assignment permutations (uniform and frequency-preserving) on a labelled additive proxy whose total error is checked against the exact quantity before use; batch-label permutations over lines; exact permutation over the 55 Broad-16 line pairs; 200-draw target and gene shuffles for the feature models. Multiplicity is reported as the number of cells screened (267: 12 strata × 2 splits; 263: ≈ 130 cells; 271: 10 + 6; 272: 11 + 9; 273: 26 arms; 286: 12; 289: 25), because a per-cell p-value ignores the screen that selected the cell. **No number in this paper is expressed in units of σ**: an earlier programme document divided per-fold deltas by a leave-one-arm-out prediction-error scale and called the ratio a σ; that language was withdrawn and one "8 σ" cell was re-measured at z = +2.3 against its proper permutation null.

**5.6 Preregistration, known-answer rows and the confirmation ledger.** Every experiment above that tests a directional claim hashed a preregistration — the statistic, the pass rule, the baselines, the controls and the required known-answer rows — before reading the outcome, and the hash is stored beside the result (F9 06:25:54Z; F10 06:57:35Z; F11 08:03:50Z; F12 08:15:18Z and the 284 rerun 13:46:19Z; C2 08:46:12Z; F14 13:54:51Z; F13 14:23:29Z; 289 14:20:21Z; C3/C4 15:13:32Z; C8 2026-09-07 16:00:01Z; C9 2026-09-07 16:27:26Z). Every screen begins with a **known-answer row** — an already-recorded number recomputed from the same code path, which must reproduce at 0.0 × 10⁰ or a stated tolerance, or the run aborts. Examples: 284 reproduced 281's primary cell at 0.0 × 10⁰; 290's primary cell rebuilt from the per-source store at a difference of 1.2 × 10⁻⁷; 283 reproduced a working line's halves and the 12-fold analysis at 0.0 × 10⁰ over 45 columns × 984 rows before any reserved number was computed; 285 reproduced four earlier medians at 0.0 × 10⁰ and one within 1.5 × 10⁻³; 291 reproduced 279's held map to 5.0 × 10⁻⁷ and 268's decomposition to 3.5 × 10⁻⁸. The discipline is only worth having if it can override a positive, and once it has: **C8 met both of its preregistered pass conditions and was nevertheless reported as `UNRESOLVABLE-AT-THIS-RESOLUTION`, because two of its seven known-answer rows failed** (§3(g)); C9 then repaired exactly those two defects and re-tested the same hypothesis at the same α, passing nine of nine — including one row, the unchanged no-signal row, that is *still* outside the threshold C8 aborted on and governs in C9 by reproduction rather than by level, because it is the defect being repaired. **Reserved data** is scored once per preregistered claim, with the result appended to `docs/CONFIRMATION-LEDGER.md` whatever it is, and the reserve then marked spent. Two riders were added by experience: independence is a property of the *cells*, not of the moment a test was frozen (this is why C1 was relabelled); and a pass rule on a multi-line or multi-study set must be depth-conditioned before it is frozen (this is why C2a's miss was predictable). Two lessons from the engineering track's known-answer rows (2026-09-27 … 09-29) generalise. First, a known-answer row must check the driver's exit status as well as the output hash: one run matched its expected hash while the driver had raised after writing it, and every later build would have died (479; found by a review from a different model, 499). Second, a gate must compare recorded values, not the presence of a record. Two reviews of the final-phase path by a different model found a post-build check that, once the final panel's download replaced the reference file, compared the new target list with itself and would have failed every correct final build (519), and a gate that passed artifacts missing the recipe's same-line record or carrying the wrong parameter values (526). Each defect was reproduced by a script that exits 0 while the defect is present, and fixed before any final-phase build.

**5.7 Leakage rules.** The H1 union source is held off the h1-test instrument, since it covers 85 of its 85 targets. PLOLO operators are fitted without the held line and every preprocessing step — operators, centring, gene filters, programme bases — is refit inside the training split. A same-target lookup across PLOLO folds leaks the held line's own response through the training folds' donor means and is excluded (+0.033 with the leak, −0.014 [−0.023, −0.005] without). In 290 and 291, features are computed on one half of the sources and evaluated on the other, and the same-half reading (+0.488 against +0.317) is reported as the leakage calibration.

Derived objects referenced above are `experiments/276-conservation-map/work/conservation276.csv` (the 300-target map), `experiments/279-znf-state-dependence/work/znf279.csv` (the 17,106-gene map over 28 sources and 21 lines), `experiments/285-reliability-benchmark/work/bench285.csv` and `analyse285.json` (the reliability benchmark), `experiments/284-conservation-family-split/work/{fam284,rem284,fit284}.json`, `experiments/290-remainder-response-features/work/fit290.json`, `experiments/291-f13-independent-surfaces/work/{surfA291,surfB291}.json`, and — for §3(g) — `experiments/307-offtarget-enrichment/work/{enrichment307.csv,results307.json,sens307.json,extra307.json}` and `experiments/309-c9-calibration-repair/work/{enrichment309.csv,results309.json,sens309.json,ka309.json}`, the per-construct tables carrying all 65 constructs. **Reserve rule:** the four Broad-16 lines JVE127, MG63, SLR23 and UMRC3 and the Orion HEK293T half-B truths are spent and are not to be used again; the eight scPerturb files of reserve R3 were declared from metadata only and scored exactly once, for claim C5 written into the ledger before the first byte of expression data was read (295, 2026-09-06 16:14–16:15Z); they are now spent and are not to be used again for a claim.
